### Organic–Inorganic Combined Application: A Review of Long-Term Experimental Insights and Pathway to Sustainable Sugarcane Production

This review synthesizes evidence from long-term field experiments, meta-analyses, and high-throughput sequencing studies on combined organic–inorganic fertilization, with a focus on sustainable sugarcane production in Yunnan, China. Prolonged use of inorganic fertilizers degrades soils through acidification, loss of organic matter, and reduced microbial diversity. Integrated nutrient management sustains crop productivity while maintaining soil health, outperforming inorganic-only applications. Organic amendments, including farmyard manure and sugarcane by-products such as pressmud, vinasse, and mill ash, improve soil structure, nutrient availability, and water retention while counteracting acidification. These amendments also reshape soil microbial communities, enriching beneficial taxa, suppressing pathogens, and increasing network stability, alongside functional shifts in N fixation, P and K solubilization, mycorrhizal symbioses, and plant growth promotion. Enhanced enzyme activities and functional microbial abundance drive improved C, N, and P cycling. Based on site-specific long-term trials, sugarcane industry by-products can replace 25–50% of inorganic fertilizer under conditions where environmental risks are appropriately managed. Future research should prioritize region-specific amendment strategies, precision agriculture tools, functional microbial characterization, standardized soil health indicators, and socioeconomic assessments of adoption constraints. This review provides a framework for transitioning to soil health-centric nutrient management, with implications for China’s sugar security and sustainable agriculture in Yunnan’s diverse landscapes and beyond.

### Stage-Associated Plant–Soil–Microbiome Patterns and Rhizosphere Pb–Zn Stabilization in Indocalamus decorus at a Mining Site

Indocalamus decorus is a perennial bamboo with potential for phytomanagement, but its Pb–Zn partitioning and associated microbiome under field co-contamination remain unclear. Parallel plantings were established at a Pb–Zn mining site and the provenance site. Plant nutrients, rhizosphere properties, Pb–Zn concentrations and soil fractions, and bacterial and fungal communities were evaluated across four developmental stages at the mining site; shoot initiation was also evaluated at the provenance site. At shoot initiation, mine-site plants had lower organ P concentrations, whereas root N was maintained. Rhizosphere nutrient availability was relatively high during shooting. Pb accumulated mainly in roots, while Zn was transferred more readily to culms; organ-specific bioconcentration factors for both metals remained below 1. Compared with bulk soil, rhizosphere soil contained lower DTPA-extractable Pb and Zn, smaller acid-soluble/exchangeable fractions, and larger residual fractions. Sample compartment explained 60.8% and 66.9% of bacterial and fungal community variation, respectively. Rhizosphere fungi were associated with a broader range of metal fractions and plant metal-distribution indices than other microbial compartments. These results indicate root-dominated Pb retention, reduced rhizosphere Pb–Zn lability, and compartment-specific microbial associations, supporting further evaluation of I. decorus as a candidate for phytostabilization-oriented management of Pb–Zn-contaminated mine soils.

### Metagenomic insights into dose-dependent alterations in the tobacco rhizosphere microbiome by pyrroloquinoline quinone

Pyrroloquinoline quinone (PQQ) is known to promote plant growth, yet how the rhizosphere microbiome varies with PQQ concentration remains poorly understood. We applied metagenomic sequencing to characterize the taxonomic composition and functional potential of the tobacco rhizosphere microbiome across PQQ treatments at 0.5, 1 and 2 μmol/L plus an untreated control. The rhizosphere microbiome was dominated by Pseudomonadota and Actinomycetota and differed among treatments in a concentration-associated, non-monotonic manner. The most pronounced differences were observed at 1 μmol/L, where alpha diversity showed dimension-specific variation and beta-diversity analysis resolved the most distinct community profile. Taxonomic and functional profiles varied concurrently, with Streptomyces, Sphingomonas, Rhodanobacter and Lysobacter, together with functions related to biosynthesis, energy metabolism, colonization and environmental sensing, showing higher relative abundance at this concentration. No broad-spectrum antimicrobial effect was detected at the community level, and antibiotic resistance gene abundance showed no significant differences among treatments after correction for multiple testing. These findings provide a basis for further evaluation of PQQ in modulating the rhizosphere microbiome in continuous cropping systems.
