### Soil clay content shapes the elevation distribution of soil organic carbon fractions via modulating microbial cross-kingdom interactions

Soil microbial interactions are crucial in maintaining ecosystem functions and the carbon (C) cycle. However, the regulatory mechanism of microbial cross-kingdom interactions on soil organic C (SOC) fractions remains unclear. Here, we collected soil samples along two independent elevation gradients in Zhongtiao Mountain (ZT) and Wutai Mountain (WT) in China. We used co-occurrence networks to investigate how cross-kingdom microbial interaction patterns are potentially linked to microbial metabolism and the distribution of particulate organic C (POC) and mineral-associated organic C (MAOC). We found that cross-kingdom interactions, particularly bacterial-fungal (competition) and protistan-bacterial (predation) networks, showed stronger associations with microbial metabolism than intra-kingdom interactions. Intensified cross-kingdom interactions were associated with reduced POC and MAOC by decreasing the microbial necromass accumulation coefficient (NAC) and increasing the microbial metabolic quotient (qCO 2 ) in both mountains. Further analysis showed that cross-kingdom interactions were linked to clay content, with a decrease in clay content providing more favorable habitats for fungi and protists, potentially intensifying competition and predation among microorganisms. The decrease in clay content along elevations in the ZT and its increase in the WT corresponded to contrasting elevation distributions in microbial cross-kingdom interactions, microbial metabolism (NAC and qCO 2 ), POC, and MAOC. Thus, these findings suggest a link between microbial community interactions and SOC, emphasizing that microbial cross-kingdom networks, which vary with soil texture and ecosystems, may play a key role in soil C dynamics through regulating microbial metabolism.

### Responses of soil characteristics and bacterial communities to agricultural irrigation with different treatment levels of wastewater

This study aimed to investigate the effects of domestic wastewater treated at different treatment levels on the biochemical characteristics of irrigated soils. Raw domestic wastewater was subjected to four treatment processes: (1) grit sedimentation; (2) grit sedimentation combined with ultrafiltration (UF); (3) grit sedimentation combined with anaerobic activated sludge treatment followed by UF; and (4) grit sedimentation combined with aerobic activated sludge treatment followed by UF. The resulting effluents (designated TWW1–TWW4) were used to irrigate soil cultivated with spinach, with tap water irrigation and non-irrigated soil serving as controls. After 50 days of irrigation, soil biochemical properties were determined. TWW1 and TWW2 exhibited COD and fecal coliform concentrations exceeding the Chinese irrigation water quality standards (GB5084-2021), whereas TWW3 and TWW4 met all stipulated requirements. TWW irrigation generally increased soil organic carbon content and electrical conductivity while decreasing soil pH, with the extent of pH reduction depending on the NH₄⁺-N concentration in the TWW. Microbial diversity was higher in TWW-irrigated soils than in controls. Specifically, genera involved in nitrogen cycling (e.g., Rhizobium , Nitrospira , and Nitrosospira ) and complex organic compound degradation (e.g., Vicinamibacteria and Blastocatellia ) were significantly enriched. This study provides valuable insights for optimizing wastewater treatment strategies to achieve safe and sustainable agricultural reuse.

### Nano‐Biochar‐Assisted Intercropping Alleviates Fusarium Wilt of Faba Bean by Enhancing Rhizosphere Soil Health in Long‐Term Continuous Cropping Soil

ABSTRACT Long‐term faba bean ( Vicia faba L.) monocropping degrades soil and promotes Fusarium wilt. Intercropping and nano‐biochar (N‐BC) can improve soil health and suppress soil‐borne diseases. However, their combined effects on Fusarium wilt in faba bean remain unclear. This study assessed whether combining intercropping with N‐BC could improve faba bean rhizosphere soil health and reduce Fusarium wilt under prolonged continuous cropping. A pot experiment was conducted to compare faba bean monocropping with faba bean–wheat intercropping under an unamended control, biochar (BC), and N‐BC treatments. Fusarium wilt incidence and rhizosphere physicochemical and biological properties were assessed. Intercropping reduced disease incidence and disease index by 8.57%–34.53% and 8.32%–30.20%, respectively. Under intercropping, BC reduced these measures by 14.95% and 15.17% compared with the control. N‐BC further reduced them by 50.02% and 54.74% compared with BC. The combined treatment increased soil water content, macroaggregate proportion, organic matter, dissolved organic carbon, ammonium nitrogen, nitrate nitrogen, available phosphorus, sucrase, urease, neutral phosphatase, catalase, and polyphenol oxidase. It decreased bulk density and benzoic, cinnamic, p‐hydroxybenzoic, ferulic, and vanillic acids. These changes coincided with higher relative abundances of potentially beneficial bacteria, including Pseudomonas , and a lower relative abundance of Fusarium . Combining faba bean – wheat intercropping with N‐BC amendment mitigated Fusarium wilt while improving rhizosphere soil structure, nutrient status, and enzyme activity, reducing phenolic acid accumulation, and reshaping microbial composition. These findings support a soil‐health‐based strategy for sustainable continuous cropping management.

### Pseudomonas sp. JP1-10 Promotes Peanut Growth and Root Branching Across Greenhouse Salinity Conditions

Microbial inoculation may support crop growth under salinity, but general growth promotion must be distinguished from salinity-dependent responses. Pseudomonas sp. JP1-10 was evaluated in greenhouse-grown peanut at 0, 2.5, and 5 g NaCl kg−1 dry soil. Growth, antioxidant responses, and rhizosphere properties were assessed at 30 and 60 days; root architecture and tissue ions were assessed at 60 days, and reproductive fresh weights were assessed at maturity. Inoculation was associated with greater shoot and root fresh weights and longer primary roots across the salinity gradient. Root tip number increased by 57.49–86.52% and lateral root number by 95.33–107.23%, with salinity-dependent branching responses. Recorded shoot and root ion values suggested lower Na and higher K and K/Na mass ratios under salinity, although their conversion to absolute tissue concentrations requires validation. Antioxidant responses varied with tissue and sampling time. In saline treatments, rhizosphere available K and Fe were higher and electrical conductivity was lower with inoculation. Pod fresh weight increased overall by 1.07 g plant−1, without a salinity × inoculation interaction. Hundred-pod and hundred-kernel fresh weights were higher with inoculation at all three salinities, with salinity × inoculation interactions. These exploratory findings support general growth promotion and identify root branching and pod fresh weight as prominent responses associated with inoculation.

### Molecular Basis of Silicon‐Mediated Plant Defense: Transporters, Cell Wall Integrity, Redox‐Hormone Crosstalk, and Epigenetic Priming

ABSTRACT Silicon (Si) is emerging as a key modulator, and its molecular mechanism of defense regulation keeps emerging. This review focus on Si‐uptake by Lsi‐type transporters, deposition in cuticles and cell wall, redox buffering, modify cell wall integrity (CWI) and hormonal signaling to increase resistance against adverse conditions and to reshape plant‐microbe‐soil interaction in rhizosphere. By highlighting the emerging research on Si‐mediated regulations at molecular scale, we proposed two working hypotheses which are ‘mechanical buffer model’ for soluble‐Si and ‘dynamic signaling model’ for Si‐nanomaterials (Si‐NPs). Mechanical buffer model states that Si‐fortified walls could stabilize the ROS profile and wall mechanics to maintain and primed state for cell while dynamic signaling model states that Si‐NPs follow an additional vesicle‐mediated uptake forming a corona protein, elicit ROS‐bursts, robust activation of FERONIA‐like receptors, Ca 2+ channels, and JA/SA signaling. Furthermore, association of Si‐associated chromatin and siRNAs were summarized and how these Si‐mediated alterations in DNA methylation under stress conditions could support the stress memory and defense priming. Overall, this framework could provide a molecular basis for Si‐mediated inputs and breeding approaches with the aim of climate‐resilient crops.
