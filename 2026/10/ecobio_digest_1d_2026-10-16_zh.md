### From acquisition to recycling: Vegetation restoration reshapes microbial nitrogen-cycling strategies in a semi-arid sandy land

Reestablishing nitrogen (N) cycling is critical for stabilizing ecosystems in semi-arid sandy lands, yet the microbial mechanisms driving functional reorganization during vegetation restoration remain unclear. This study investigated the coupling of soil physicochemical properties, microbial traits, and N-cycling genes across a five-stage restoration sequence in the Mu Us Sandy Land: bare sandy land (BSL), grassland (GL), shrubland (SL), grass-shrub mixed land (GSL), and forest land (FL). Restoration significantly ameliorated edaphic conditions, with soil organic carbon (SOC) increasing approximately 7-fold and pH declining from 8.57 to 6.79. Notably, the GSL stage emerged as a “biogeochemical hotspot”, exhibiting peak microbial biomass and hydrolytic enzyme activities (urease and sucrase) linked to resource heterogeneity. Metagenomic profiling revealed a functional shift in microbial strategies: genetic potential for N-fixation ( nifH/K ) and urea hydrolysis dominated in oligotrophic BSL, indicating a strong investment in N acquisition. In contrast, nitrification ( amo ) was most prominent in GL, while denitrification and anammox pathways intensified in shrub-grass mosaics (GSL). Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) and Mantel tests further demonstrated that SOC accumulation primarily drives these functional shifts by reshaping microbial biomass and community assembly. Collectively, these findings suggest that vegetation restoration transitions the soil N-cycle from an external-acquisition-dominated system to a tighter, internal-recycling regime, with mixed vegetation stages acting as critical functional transitions.

### Potato straw incorporation improves soybean agronomic traits and yield components and is associated with shifts in rhizosphere soil properties microbial communities and metabolites

Background The soil-improving effects of cereal straw incorporation have been well documented; however, potato straw differs markedly from cereal straw in composition and degradation characteristics, and whether straw return is associated with the soil microbial ecosystem and with gains in yield components through pathways similar to those of cereal straw remains unclear. Methods In this study, a field-embedded pot experiment was conducted to characterize the interaction network among soil physicochemical properties, microbial communities, and metabolites under potato straw incorporation, and to elucidate its regulatory effects on soybean growth and yield components. Results Compared with no incorporation, straw incorporation significantly increased soil organic matter (by 21.60%), available potassium (35.39%), alkali-hydrolyzable nitrogen (16.94%), and available phosphorus (15.42%), and significantly enhanced the activities of acid phosphatase (28.63%) and polyphenol oxidase (16.04%). Amplicon sequencing identified treatment-associated enrichment of bacterial and fungal taxa previously linked to residue decomposition, nutrient cycling (e.g., Paenibacillus and Nitrosomonadaceae ), or plant-beneficial interactions(e.g., arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi [Glomeromycota] and Bionectria ). Correlation analysis between the soil metabolome and the microbial community showed that both bacterial and fungal communities exhibited a dichotomous pattern of contrasting metabolite preferences: among bacteria, Proteobacteria-dominated taxa were positively correlated with organic acid metabolites, whereas taxa dominated by Gemmatimonadota, Firmicutes, and Actinobacteria were positively correlated with aromatic amino acids and nitrogen-containing heterocyclic compounds, each group being negatively correlated with the metabolites preferred by the other; among fungi, root-symbiotic taxa were positively correlated with secondary metabolites such as flavonoids, whereas saprotrophic fungi were positively correlated with plant-residue decomposition markers such as 2-furoic acid and geranylgeraniol. Three relatively independent organic nitrogen transformation pathways—arginine metabolism, histidine degradation, and purine nucleoside catabolism–were also detected, and pH, organic matter, urease (S-UE), and soybean isoflavone metabolites (daidzein and genistin) showed strong co-occurrence patterns with other network members. Conclusion Soybean plant height, stem diameter, pods per plant, and 100-seed weight significantly increased by 14.93%, 17.77%, 19.10%, and 11.40%, respectively. In summary, potato straw incorporation broadened the soil organic nitrogen substrate spectrum by reshaping the soil physicochemical property–microbe–metabolite interaction network, thereby promoting soybean growth and yield components formation through multi-pathway synergy. These findings provide a theoretical basis for the resource utilization of potato straw.

### Rotation system reshapes soil profile environmental gradients to reorganize bacterial communities and decouple potential nitrogen cycling functions

Inroduction Cropping systems reshape soil profile environmental gradients, thereby driving the vertical reorganization of bacterial communities and regulating the coupling between microbial communities and predicted nitrogen-cycling (N-cycling) potential. Methods This study integrated soil chemical analyses, 16S rRNA gene sequencing, co-occurrence network analysis, PICRUSt2-based functional prediction, and structural equation modeling (SEM) across three long-term cropping systems: unclaimed land (U), tobacco–maize rotation (R), and continuous tobacco cropping (C), sampled at three soil depths (0–20, 20–40, and 40–60 cm; n = 3). Results Environmental drivers exhibited distinct vertical shifts, with readily available nutrients (AN, AK, and AP) dominating surface soils (0–20 cm), microbial biomass (MBC and MBN) governing subsurface soils (20–40 cm), and total nutrient pools (OM, TN, and TP) becoming increasingly important in deeper soils (40–60 cm). Rotation buffered vertical environmental changes, increased bacterial richness in the 0–40 cm profile, enriched nitrifying and nitrogen-fixing taxa (e.g., Nitrospira, Sphingomonas, and MND1), and maintained higher predicted N-cycling potential throughout the soil profile, accompanied by enhanced network modularity and robustness. In contrast, continuous cropping reduced surface soil pH, OM, MBC, and MBN (with MBN declining by>60%), promoted stress-associated taxa (e.g., Bacillus and Lactobacillus), and weakened network connectivity and robustness, particularly at 20–40 cm where network fragmentation was most evident. SEM revealed a stronger statistical coupling among soil environment, bacterial communities, microbial networks, and predicted N-cycling potential under rotation (GOF > 0.7), whereas continuous cropping showed weakened associations among these components. Discussion Collectively, these findings demonstrate that crop rotation promotes the stability of soil microbial ecological functions by preserving environmental gradient continuity and enhancing network resilience, whereas continuous cropping disrupts microbial network organization and weakens the linkage between resource availability and predicted functional potential. This study highlights the importance of maintaining multiscale environmental and microbial connectivity for improving the sustainability of agricultural soil ecosystems.

### Beneficial Soil Microorganisms and Their Role in Phytopathological Biotechnology

The complex relationship between plants and soil microorganisms has been extensively investigated in scientific studies, revealing a dynamic interplay that significantly impacts plant health and productivity. This chapter explores the importance of beneficial soil microorganisms in the field of phytopatho-logical biotechnology. These microscopic allies, including bacteria, fungi, and archaea, have become essential players in promoting plant growth and providing resistance against various pathogens. The interactions between beneficial soil microorganisms and plants are diverse, involving mechanisms, such as nutrient cycling, biocontrol, and induced systemic resistance. Key microbial actors, such as mycorrhizal fungi and plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria, are emphasized for their ability to improve nutrient absorption, promote root development, and suppress pathogenic invaders. Additionally, the intricate signaling networks and molecular dialogues between plants and these microorganisms are explored, revealing the sophisticated strategies 182 employed by plants to utilize the protective potential of their microbial partners. Phytopathological biotechnology utilizes these intricate relationships to develop sustainable and environmentally friendly approaches for managing diseases in crops. The use of beneficial microorganisms as biopesticides and biofertilizers represents a promising avenue for reducing dependence on traditional chemical inputs. Moreover, the integration of modern biotechnological tools, such as genomics and metagenomics, aids in comprehensively understanding the diverse microbial communities in the rhizosphere and their functional roles in promoting plant health. In a nutshell, this chapter underscores the pivotal role of beneficial soil microorganisms in shaping the landscape of phytopathological biotechnology. Leveraging the capabilities of these microbial allies not only contributes to sustainable agriculture but also has the potential to revolutionize strategies for crop protection, fostering a harmonious coexistence between plants and their hidden guardians in the soil.

### Metabolomics-based comparison of metabolite profiles in Capparis spinosa L. from different habitats and associated soil factors

Introduction Capparis spinosa L. is a distinctive plant with edible and medicinal value, yet the factors influencing the accumulation of its effective components across diverse habitats remain unclear. This study aims to profile fruit metabolites and explore the association with rhizosphere soil environments. Methods Using LC-MS/MS-based untargeted metabolomics (six biological replicates per habitat), fruit metabolites from three habitats in northern Xinjiang (Urumqi, Karamay, and Ili) were profiled. Partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) identified differential metabolites. 16S rRNA sequencing analyzed rhizosphere soil microbial communities, and correlation analyses linked fruit metabolites with soil metabolites and microbiota. Results A total of 1,739 metabolites were identified. PLS-DA revealed clear habitat separation, with 454, 340, and 411 differential metabolites in pairwise comparisons. Sixteen anti-inflammatory compounds (flavonoids, terpenoids, etc.) were candidate quality-associated metabolites, predominantly highest in Urumqi. Soil bacterial/fungal composition differed significantly among habitats, with Rubrobacterand Gaiella enriched in Urumqi. Correlation analysis linked 16 fruit metabolites (e.g., resveratrol, kaempferol) to 10 soil metabolites and specific soil genera. Discussion Habitat-driven differences in soil microbial communities and metabolite levels are associated with bioactive component abundance in C. spinosa fruit, offering targets for habitat selection and microbiome-informed cultivation. Note that LC-MS/MS signal intensities are semi-quantitative; reported differences reflect relative abundance rather than absolute content.

### Complementary root and mycorrhizal foraging strategies for phosphorus acquisition among arbuscular and ectomycorrhizal trees in a subtropical forest

Abstract Phosphorus (P) availability is widely recognized as an important constraint on plant growth in many subtropical forest ecosystems, yet how contrasting mycorrhizal strategies mediate the use of inorganic and organic P forms remains unclear. In a subtropical forest, we integrated a 50‐ha survey of adult tree dominance, a seedling growth survey across six 1‐ha plots and a shade‐house experiment to examine how arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) and ectomycorrhizal (ECM) trees adjust root‐fungal foraging strategies across gradients of inorganic and organic P availability. In the field, quadrats with higher inorganic P availability were dominated by AM trees, whereas ECM trees were dominant in soils with greater organic P availability. In both the field survey and the shade‐house experiment, the growth of AM seedlings increased with inorganic P availability, whereas that of ECM seedlings increased with organic P availability. The results of structural equation modelling showed that, in AM seedlings, inorganic P enhanced specific root area and mycorrhizal colonization, thereby increasing root P concentration and biomass. In ECM seedlings, organic P increased mycorrhizal colonization and acid phosphatase activity, elevating root P concentration and biomass. Our findings reveal functional complementarity between AM and ECM tree species in nutrient acquisition strategies. By providing a trait‐based framework, we offer mechanistic insight into variation in seedling performance and community assembly along soil P gradients in subtropical forests.
