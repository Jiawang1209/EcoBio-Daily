## 农田土壤微生物

### Natural isolates of Sinorhizobium meliloti with dry, as opposed to mucoid, colony morphologies have higher soil fitness, slightly lower host fitness, and reduced nitrogen fixation

Sinorhizobium meliloti is a nitrogen-fixing soil bacterium that forms a facultative mutualism with its host alfalfa. S. meliloti strains exhibit diverse colony morphologies on solid culture media that span a continuum from dry to mucoid. Studies on how this trait affects symbiosis rely on mutations in exopolysaccharide II production genes; however, whether natural variation in morphology confers a selective advantage on rhizobia in their natural habitats remains unknown. Here, we used 90 mucoid and 27 dry natural isolates to test whether colony morphology is associated with rhizobial fitness in the soil and in the host, or with the nitrogen-fixation benefits they provide to the plant. Colony morphologies were widely distributed across a phylogenetic tree, suggesting that morphological shifts occurred repeatedly and no single genetic variant was strongly associated with this trait. We inoculated soil mesocosms and plants with a mixture of dry and mucoid bacteria. We observed that dry rhizobia have higher fitness, particularly at 22{degrees}C and 32{degrees}C, but not under cold or drought conditions, independent of the initial rhizobial load. Conversely, mucoid bacteria have a modestly higher relative fitness in host nodules, and single inoculations showed that they tend to confer greater benefits to all three alfalfa varieties. Over time, we also observed rare, repeated conversion from mucoid to dry morphologies in soil and host plants. Taken together, our study reveals that morphological variation within a species in a single agricultural field is important for bacterial fitness in the soil and can have cascading effects on the rhizobia's plant benefits.

### Bioprocess Engineering for Enhanced Zinc Solubilization Through Response Surface Methodology (RSM) by Newly Isolated Rhizobacteria

Zinc (Zn) is an essential micronutrient for optimum plant growth; however, Zn deficiency is widespread in agricultural soils due to the low availability of plant-accessible or soluble forms of Zn. Therefore, harnessing microorganisms’ ability to solubilize Zn can be a significant, environmentally beneficial contribution to sustainable agriculture. This study isolated Zn-solubilizing bacteria (ZnSB) from rhizosphere soil and assessed their capacity to solubilize Zn minerals (ZnO, ZnCO3). Based on qualitative and quantitative evaluation, the highest solubilization efficiency (345%) and 954 ± 46 mg Zn L−1, respectively, were observed in the AEYTU11 strain and were associated with a strong pH decrease in the culture medium. Based on phenotypic characterization and 16S rRNA sequence analysis, strain AEYTU11 was identified as Serratia marcescens. The in vitro solubilization potential of microbes is significantly influenced by process parameters, emphasizing the importance of optimizing the culture conditions to maximize microbial solubilization efficiency. A Box–Behnken design (BBD) experiment with 17 combinations of varying levels of insoluble Zn compound (0.5–1.5 g/L), pH (5–9), and incubation time (3–5 days) was used to model their effects on Serratia marcescens AEYTU11 strain-mediated Zn release from ZnCO3. Following process optimization, the solubilization efficiency of the AEYTU11 strain increased, achieving a maximum Zn solubilization capacity of 1608 ± 51 mg/L. The resulting model had high predictive ability (R2 = 0.9099) and showed that ZnCO3 concentrations were the most significant factor for the amounts of Zn released. In addition to Zn solubilization, AEYTU11 possess potassium (K) solubilization and extracellular enzyme production for protease and cellulose activity. The optimization findings highlight the value of statistical optimization tools for maximizing microbial Zn-solubilizing activity and improving biofertilizer development.

### Effects of Combined Peat Powder and Fermented Manure on Oat Yield, Soil Enzyme Activities and Microbial Community Composition in Horqin Sandy Land

Low soil fertility caused by organic matter deficit severely restricts crop productivity in sandy lands. To investigate the effects of organic matter addition on plant yield and soil productivity in sandy areas, a two-factor randomized block field experiment was conducted using oat (Avena sativa) with five ratios (7:3, 3:2, 1:1, 2:3, 3:7; w:w) and five application rates (0, 5, 10, 15, and 20, t·ha−1) of peat powder and fermented manure in the Horqin Sandy Land, which is a typical agro-pastoral ecotone in northern China. Application of combined peat powder and fermented manure significantly increased oat yield, and the fertilization rate exerted a stronger effect on oat yield than the mixing ratio of the two organic materials. Furthermore, organic fertilization significantly increased the relative abundances of microorganisms, improved soil C, N and P contents, and enhanced the activities of urease, invertase, and catalase. The combination of an application rate of 20 t·ha−1 and a 3:2 peat-powder-to-fermented-manure ratio represents a promising fertilization regime among treatments tested for oat cultivation in this region, given its highest aboveground dry-matter yield. Collectively, these findings show that rational combined application of fermented manure and peat powder in sandy land can significantly increase soil enzyme activities and optimize soil nutrient stoichiometry, and is positively correlated with rhizosphere microbial community composition and oat yield.

## 森林土壤微生物

### Soil metabarcoding reveals the distinct microbial signature of a remnant forest patch at a local restoration initiative in the Ecuadorian Chocó

The Tumbes-Chocó-Magdalena hotspot is a globally recognized center of endemism that is undergoing severe deforestation. While conservation and restoration efforts typically prioritize the recovery of aboveground macroscopic biodiversity, the response of soil microbial communities remains largely uncharacterized in this region. To address this data gap, we assessed soil bacterial communities in three land-use types (reference forest, early-stage restored parcel, and degraded land) within the "La Esperanza Forest" reserve in the Ecuadorian Chocó. Using 16S rRNA gene metabarcoding and robust compositional data analysis (e.g., ALDEx2, PERMANOVA), we evaluated shifts in taxonomic diversity and community structure. Sequencing revealed 1,208 genera and dominant genera (e.g., Bacillus , Paenibacillus ) that persisted across all habitats. However, despite the shared presence of these dominant taxa, the sampled land-use plots exhibited distinct structural differences in their microbial communities. Reference remnant forest's soils exhibited significantly higher community evenness and distinct multivariate partitioning driven by strongly associated structural taxa (e.g., Dinghuibacter , Phyllobacterium ). Conversely, degraded lands were significantly enriched in stress-associated bacteria, notably Rhodanobacter . Early-stage restored parcel soil clustered structurally closer to degraded soil than to the reference forest, indicating a pronounced spatial compositional similarity to disturbed soils rather than the reference state. Our findings from this localized study indicate that the fundamental microbial scaffolding of this remnant Chocó Forest soil hosts a distinct bacterial community structure, and that recovering soil microbial diversity in the adjacent old agricultural land may require extended timeframes and additional restoration approaches. We highlight remnant forest patches as critical reservoirs of belowground biodiversity and emphasize the necessity of integrating soil microbiology into continuous, long-term conservation monitoring frameworks.

### Nitrogen addition alters soil microbial community structure and decrease nitrogen cycling gene abundance in acidic macadamia orchard soils

Nitrogen addition can alter soil microbial communities and regulate nitrogen cycling processes. However, the role of nitrogen-induced soil acidification in modulating nitrogen cycling in macadamia orchard soils remains poorly understood. Based on a 3-year field fertilization experiment, this study established three treatments: no-nitrogen (N0), medium-nitrogen (N1), and high-nitrogen (N2). We systematically investigate the effects of nitrogen addition on soil chemical properties, the abundance of nitrogen cycling genes, enzyme activities, microbial community structure, and co-occurrence networks. The results showed that nitrogen addition significantly increased the contents of alkaline hydrolyzable nitrogen (AHN) and nitrate nitrogen (NO 3 – -N), altered soil stoichiometric ratios, and exacerbated soil acidification. Quantitative PCR (qPCR) analysis revealed that nitrogen addition significantly decreased the abundance of nitrification genes (AOA- amoA ), ammonification gene ( ureC ), and denitrification genes ( narG , nirK , nosZ ), while no significant changes were observed for AOB- amoA or nirS . 16S rRNA gene sequencing demonstrated that nitrogen addition altered the succession of microbial communities towards a copiotrophic (R-strategy) state and altered the predicted carbon-metabolism functional profiles. Co-occurrence network analysis showed that the N2 treatment reduced network complexity but enhanced modularity, suggesting that microbial communities may respond to nitrogen addition through functional modular reorganization. The asynchronous changes between key enzyme activities and their corresponding gene abundances revealed the complex regulatory mechanisms of the nitrogen cycle. Furthermore, correlation analyses identified pH, electrical conductivity (EC), and NO 3 – -N were important environmental factors affecting nitrogen cycle function gene groups. These findings provide new insights into the response mechanisms of the nitrogen cycle in the soil ecosystem of Macadamia orchards.

### Soil Organic Matter Pools Vary Across Management Systems but Weakly Predict Short-Term Nitrogen Cycling

The dynamics of soil carbon and nitrogen are important for soil fertility and ecosystem functioning, and agricultural management and land use strongly affect them. However, it is still not clear how different management systems change soil organic carbon (SOC) and soil organic nitrogen (SON), or if larger SOC and SON pools go together with more available nitrogen through mineralization and nitrification. The data come from the Kellogg Biological Station Main Cropping System Experiment; this site has SOC and SON measurements for several years and depths, and its nitrogen cycling variables were monitored at 0–25 cm depth. Treatment comparison and linear regression were used to test the differences among systems and the relationships between soil organic matter pools and nitrogen cycling. SOC and SON differed significantly among the treatments; the long successional system had the highest concentrations and the conventional system the lowest. Nitrogen cycling had different treatment patterns, and only SON had a significant positive relationship with net nitrification (R² = 0.16, p = 0.007); the other three regressions were not significant. The analysis relied on one experimental site, and the datasets matched only partly in time; therefore, the results cannot be generalized very far. In general, management had a strong effect on SOC and SON, but these pools by themselves predicted short-term nitrogen cycling only weakly.
