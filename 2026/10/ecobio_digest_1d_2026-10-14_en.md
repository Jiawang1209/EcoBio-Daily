### The effects of co-application of organic fertilizer and limestone powder on soil organic carbon and its fractions in rubber plantations: associations with microbial necromass carbon and Fe/Al oxides

Introduction Rubber plantations serve as significant carbon reservoirs within China’s terrestrial ecosystems. To provide a scientific foundation for improving soil fertility management, this study investigated the response of soil organic carbon and its components in rubber plantations to different soil amendments. Methods Soil samples were collected from the 0–20 cm horizon of acidic soils in second-generation rubber plantations in Jinghong City, Yunnan Province. A laboratory incubation experiment was then conducted with four treatments: an unamended control (CK), organic fertilizer alone (T1), limestone powder alone (T2), and a combined organic fertilizer and limestone powder treatment (T3). The effects of these amendments on soil organic carbon and its fractions, iron and aluminum oxides, and microbial necromass carbon were systematically evaluated. Results The results demonstrated that under T3, Organic carbon (SOC) increased significantly by 12.80%, relative to CK ( p < 0.05). Treatments T2 and T3 significantly enhanced microbial necromass carbon (by 6.92–10.90%), fungal necromass carbon (by 6.60–11.32%), and bacterial necromass carbon (by 7.79–9.74%) compared to CK ( p < 0.05). T3 also significantly increased amorphous (Fe o , Al o ) and complexed (Fe p , Al p ) iron and aluminum oxides (p < 0.05). Two-way ANOVA revealed a significant interaction between organic fertilizer and limestone powder on POC, MAOC, MNC, and oxide forms, indicating an interactive regulatory effect on the distribution of carbon fractions and the transformation of mineral forms. Regression and correlation analyses showed microbial necromass carbon and Fe/Al oxides significantly positively correlated with SOC and its fractions, suggesting close associations among necromass accumulation, oxide protection, and SOC fraction changes. VPA showed that the shared explanatory power of microbial and mineral variables for SOC stabilization exceeded their respective pure effects. Conclusion In summary, microbial necromass carbon, iron and aluminum oxides, and pH are closely related to soil organic carbon and its components. The combined application of organic fertilizer and limestone powder is highly effective in mitigating soil acidification and increasing SOC, microbial necromass carbon, and iron and aluminum oxides, and can serve as a reference for soil fertility management and carbon pool regulation in acidified soils of tropical rubber plantations.

### Soil Organic Carbon Dynamics and Carbon Management Index under Forest and Agricultural Land-Use Systems in the Western Himalayas

Himalayan soils are highly susceptible to the impacts of climate change and land-use shifts. Soil organic carbon (SOC) and the carbon management index (CMI) are key measures of soil health in response to land use changes. This study assessed total organic carbon (TOC), carbon fractions, carbon pool index (CPI), CMI, and physicochemical properties in forest and agricultural land-use systems in the temperate western Himalayas. Soil samples were collected across 32 distinct sites representing forest (FS), wheat (WS), rice (RS), and maize (MS) systems (n = 32). FS exhibited the highest average TOC (25.6 g kg-1), followed by MS (19.3 g kg-1), WS (15.2 g kg-1), and RS (14 g kg-1). TOC levels differed significantly between land-use categories (ANOVA; F = 13.4, P< 0.001), with all agricultural systems showing significantly lower TOC levels than forests. The maximum concentration of labile carbon (LC) was identified in FS (4.3%), while the lowest was observed in WS (2.8%). MS had the highest CPI (0.75), whereas RS had the highest average CMI (85.5), indicating distinct carbon management dynamics despite lower baseline TOC. TOC was positively correlated with altitude and electrical conductivity but negatively correlated with soil pH. Multiple linear regression (R2 = 0.70) confirmed land-use type as the primary predictor of TOC variation, showing significant reductions for RS (11.6 g kg-1), WS (10.7 g kg-1), and MS (4.9 g kg-1) compared to the forest baseline (P< 0.05). Principal component analysis successfully differentiated agricultural and forest systems based on topography, soil properties, and carbon pools. The primary cause of SOC depletion is the initial conversion of forests to farmland, underscoring the need for climate smart land management to maintain ecosystem services and replenish regional carbon stocks.

### Plant Growth-Promoting Rhizobacteria: Role in the Detoxification of Heavy Metals in Plants and Bioremediation of Agricultural Soils

Heavy metal contamination of agricultural soils is a critical environmental challenge because of its persistence and the risks it poses to ecosystems and human health. Heavy metals cannot be degraded, and changes in soil conditions can remobilize stable fractions into labile, bioavailable forms. Plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR) are increasingly used as bioinoculants that assist phytoremediation rather than as a stand-alone technology. Depending on the strain, host plant and soil conditions, PGPR can either mobilize metals and enhance their uptake and translocation (phytoextraction) or immobilize them in the rhizosphere and roots, reducing their transfer to aboveground tissues (phytostabilization). This critical review, supported by a structured and reproducible literature search, synthesizes the evidence on (i) plant growth-promoting traits under metal stress; (ii) direct PGPR–metal interactions, including biosorption by extracellular polymeric substances, metallothioneins, organic acids, redox transformation and microbially induced carbonate precipitation; (iii) the regulation of plant antioxidant defenses; and (iv) root exudate-driven, endophyte-assisted and formulation-based strategies. The evidence, dominated by short-term pot experiments with single metals, is critically compared, and priorities for field validation, mixed contamination and regulatory approval are identified.

### Root exudates enhance the ecological fitness of a non-native PGPR and reshape rhizosphere microbiome assembly

Root exudates play critical roles in rhizosphere microbiome assembly. Nevertheless, the molecular mechanisms by which plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR) perceive these signals, adapt to the rhizosphere, and influence native microbial communities remain poorly understood. Here, using maize (Zea mays cv. Early Golden Bantam) and the rhizobacterium Priestia aryabhattai AB211, we took a multi-omics approach combining metabolomics, bacterial transcriptomics and proteomics, and rhizosphere metagenomics to elucidate the mechanism underlying this process. Maize root exudates were enriched in diverse carbon and nitrogen metabolites that promoted bacterial growth and induced extensive molecular reprogramming of pathways involved in nutrient acquisition, chemotaxis, motility, stress adaptation, and plant growth promotion, thereby enhancing rhizosphere competence. In field-conditioned soil, AB211 persisted as a core member of the maize rhizosphere microbiome, promoted early seedling growth, and reshaped microbial community composition and function. Root exudates-primed (RE-primed) AB211 further enhanced the enrichment of microbial functions associated with nutrient cycling, quorum sensing, plant growth promotion, and rhizosphere fitness, highlighting the role of root exudates in modulating the rhizosphere-adaptative potential of beneficial bacteria. These findings establish a mechanistic framework linking root exudates composition with PGPR adaptation and microbiome assembly, providing a foundation for engineering ecologically adapted microbial inoculants to improve crop productivity and sustainable agriculture.

### Exploration of Algal Diversity: Phytochemical Analysis and Utilisation as Sustainable Bio- Fertilizer From Paddy Fields of Simdega, Jharkhand

The algal and cyanobacterial communities thriving in the paddy-field ecosystems serve as good indicators of the wetland's suitability for the growth of other organisms and can potentially be applied in sustainable agriculture. The main objectives of the present study was to study the diversity of algae present in the paddy fields of Simdega, Jharkhand, to determine their phytochemical and nutrient profile and to determine their potential use as biofertilizer in paddy field. The secondary data was obtained from the published research articles, scientific publications, institutional publications, government reports and official government websites. Systematic compilation and descriptive analysis of information related to algal diversity, biochemical constituents, nitrogen fixation, nutrient cycling, soil fertility and cultivation of paddy were carried out and agricultural characteristics of Simdega were discussed. The available evidence suggests that paddy fields can harbour cyanobacteria, green algae, diatoms and other algal groups and their distribution depends on water availability, soil characteristics, nutrients, temperature and agriculture. Proteins, carbohydrates, amino acids, nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, chlorophyll, carotenoids and phycobiliproteins have been reported in the freshwater algae from regional studies of Jharkhand. Nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria can supply biologically available nitrogen, algal standing crop can supply organic matter and nutrients, and may also affect soil microbial community activity and plant growth. The results suggest that indigenous algal resources can be used as sustainable alternative for nutrient management in the paddy cultivation system. However, their effectiveness as bio-fertilizers in the field needs to be confirmed through species specific laboratory characterization and controlled field experiments for their efficiency in Simdega.

### Cobamide-based interactions between soil bacteria can be predicted based on monoculture growth

ABSTRACT Interactions between microbes shape the structure and function of microbial communities. While studying interactions is key to understanding microbial communities as a whole, gaining a detailed mechanistic view is challenging due to the number of co-occurring interactions. Here, we focus on a single class of shared nutrients, the cobalamin (vitamin B 12 ) family of enzyme cofactors known as cobamides, to study nutrient interactions in laboratory consortia of increasing complexity. Measuring cobamide-dependent growth of two soil Caulobacter isolates in monoculture revealed that each grows faster within a distinct range of cobamide concentrations. Co-culturing the two isolates demonstrated that the cobamide concentration predictably determines which isolate predominates. These results suggest that, although the two organisms appear functionally redundant, with identical metabolic capabilities predicted in their genomes, they may occupy distinct niches in their environment based on cobamide concentration. We further examined the ability of cobamide-producing Mesorhizobium and Priestia isolates to share cobamides and found that both support the cobamide-dependent growth of the two Caulobacter isolates. The Caulobacter isolates could coexist in tri-cultures with each producer following serial passaging, indicating the producers can support the growth of multiple cobamide-dependent organisms. We analyzed the metabolic capacity encoded in the genomes of the four isolates and found that cobamides are likely the main shared nutrient in our co- and tri-cultures. These results highlight the utility of cobamides as a model nutrient to characterize and predict interactions in bacterial consortia of increasing complexity. IMPORTANCE Interactions between microbes shape their communities and the world around them. Because microbial communities are physically and chemically complex, predicting microbial interactions is often difficult. Using a set of four bacteria isolated from the same soil environment, we generated and tested predictions about the interactions involving a family of model nutrients related to cobalamin (vitamin B 12 ). Understanding and predicting interactions can lead to greater insights into microbiome function.
