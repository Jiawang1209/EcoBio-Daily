### Microbial mechanisms underlying the shift of net global warming potential in a maize field under organic amendments

While organic amendments are promoted for enhancing soil quality, their impacts on the net global warming potential (GWP) and the underlying microbial mechanisms in the maize system remain elusive. By using a long-term field experiment established in 2007 under a winter wheat-summer maize rotation system, a field study was conducted to investigate the effects of different organic-amended treatments (manure application and straw return coupled with conventional or no tillage) on greenhouse gas (i.e., nitrous oxide [N 2 O] and carbon dioxide [CO 2 ]) emissions, soil organic carbon (SOC) stocks and functional genes governing soil C and N cycling during the 2021 and 2022 maize growing seasons. Compared to conventional N fertilization (CN), long-term organic amendments, particularly manure application, significantly enhanced SOC stocks by 16.7–46.2% while reducing cumulative N 2 O emissions by 22.9–30.2%, respectively. Although a 22.6–44.5% increase in CO 2 emissions were observed under organic amendments relative to CN, the concurrent SOC gains and N 2 O mitigation shifted the field-scale net GWP from positive to negative. Multivariate analyses implied that enhanced SOC sequestration under organic amendments was mainly associated with greater exogenous organic matter inputs and enriched C fixation genes, and lower N 2 O emissions were accompanied by lower bacterial amoA and higher nosZ gene abundances, suggesting that reduced nitrification and enhanced N 2 O reduction potential may have contributed to N 2 O mitigation. Therefore, long-term manure application is a viable strategy to achieve climate-smart maize production by enhancing SOC sequestration, mitigating N 2 O emissions and maintaining yield without additional chemical N input.

### Screening and Characterization of Drought and Salt-tolerant Plant Growth Promoting Rhizobacteria from Wheat Rhizosphere

Drought and salinity are major environmental constraints affecting wheat growth and yield. Therefore, there is a need for sustainable approaches to improve crop performance under adverse conditions. In this study, indigenous bacteria associated with the wheat rhizosphere were isolated from the Malwa and Nimar regions of Madhya Pradesh, India, to identify strains capable of tolerating drought- and salt-related stress. A total of 150 bacterial isolates were obtained from rhizospheric soil samples collected from Indore, Khargone, and Maheshwar. Of these, 20 representative isolates were selected and evaluated for their morphological, biochemical, physiological, and plant growth-promoting characteristics. The isolates were assessed for tolerance to different concentrations of NaCl, polyethylene glycol (PEG)-induced osmotic stress, temperature variations, and pH conditions. Among the isolates, WR4, WR5, and WR9 retained growth at NaCl concentrations of up to 12%, while WR4 and WR9 demonstrated comparatively greater tolerance to PEG-induced osmotic stress. The isolates also exhibited several plant growth-promoting traits, including nitrogen fixation, ammonia production, phosphate and potassium solubilisation, hydrogen cyanide (HCN) production, and indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) synthesis. Phosphate-solubilising activity ranged from 31.3–39.2 mg/mL, whereas IAA production ranged from approximately 11–21 µg/mL, with WR4 showing the highest IAA production. Overall, the findings indicate that wheat rhizosphere-associated bacteria from the studied regions possess both abiotic stress tolerance and plant growth-promoting properties. These indigenous isolates may therefore represent promising candidates for microbial inoculant development for drought- and salinity-prone environments. Molecular characterisation and subsequent greenhouse and field experiments will be required to confirm their taxonomic identity, functional stability, and effectiveness under agricultural conditions.

### Study on The Effect of a Propiconazole Based Fungicide on Plant Growth Promoting Properties of Isolates from Wheat Rhizosphere

Reliance on propiconazole based fungicides has been on the rise for better harvest of crops. Such fungicides can also affect non-target rhizosphere microorganisms. However, the off-target effect of propiconazole on rhizobacteria remains scarcely discussed. The main objective of the present study was to observe the effect of this compound on six plant growth promoting (PGP) properties of wheat rhizosphere isolates using a statistical approach. The study showed that although the overall PGP profile shifted only modestly across propiconazole doses, siderophore and ammonia production were suppressed at the highest fungicide concentration, IAA production declined in the presence of the fungicide, and cellulase activity declined at 4.24 mg/L and above, whereas siderophore appeared to act as a bridge between growth promotion and enzymatic activity. This pattern indicates propiconazole exerts a selective pressure on specific traits rather than holistic disruption. The findings of this study contribute to our understanding of pesticide risk assessment in sustainable agriculture.

### Integrated microbiome and metabolome analysis reveals mechanisms of Fusarium wilt resistance in banana rhizosphere soil under different continuous cropping durations

Introduction Fusarium fungi are the primary causal agents of banana Fusarium wilt disease. In this study, using a resistant banana line (derived from cultivar Baodao), we observed that with increasing years of continuous cropping, the incidence of Fusarium wilt disease showed a notable reduction. Results Microbial community abundance was lower in the susceptible banana variety (V) than in the resistant variety [sampled after one (S1), two (S2), and three (S3) years of continuous cropping]. With increasing continuous cropping years (S1, S2, and S3), microbial abundance increased, while the abundance of Fusarium oxysporum significantly decreased, and these changes coincided with visually healthier plant growth compared to the susceptible variety. However, the relative abundance of Fusarium , Bisifusarium Gibellulopsis , and Rectifusarium were the highest in V. Rozellomycota sp., Trichoderma virens , Trichoderma asperellum , and Hypocreaceae sp. were also identified with high abundance in S2 and S3, but were not detected in V. In addition, using Spearman’s correlation analysis and network analysis (|r| > 0.6), we found that the reduction of Fusarium pathogens was significantly correlated with the enrichment of beneficial bacteria such as Sphingomonas parvus and Rozellomycota sp. A total of 1153 metabolites were identified, including amino acids, phenolic acids, organic acids, phenolic acids, and flavonols. Among these, 79 compounds showed a significant decrease in V but increased in S1, S2, and S3; these compounds were negatively correlated with Fusarium oxysporum and positively correlated with Rozellomycota sp. Additionally, 47 compounds were associated with Sphingomonas parvus , Bryobacter and other beneficial bacteria. Conclusion These findings suggest that specific metabolic compounds are associated with the enrichment of beneficial fungi and bacteria in the banana rhizosphere soil as continuous cropping time increases.

### Spatial distribution and environmental drivers of comammox Nitrospira versus canonical ammonia oxidizers in subtropical forests

Ammonia-oxidizing archaea (AOA) and ammonia-oxidizing bacteria (AOB) have been recognized as important nitrifiers in various ecosystems; however, the complete ammonia oxidation (comammox) bacteria affiliated with the genus Nitrospira are also an important contributor to soil nitrification, which has been demonstrated in agricultural and wetland ecosystems. Despite these findings, comammox Nitrospira in subtropical forest soils remain poorly understood. Here, we investigated the diversity, community composition, and abundance of comammox Nitrospira along a subtropical forest transect in Southeast China spanning ∼900 km, including coniferous and broadleaf forests at three representative sites. Most detected OTUs belonged to Clade B, with only a few assigned to Clade A2 and none to Clade A1. Comammox Nitrospira were present across all samples, with amoA gene abundances ranging from 3.14 × 10 8 to 14.9 × 10 8 copies g −1 dry soil, accounting for 21.9%-56.6% of the total amoA gene copies. Their abundance decreased with increasing latitude and was higher in broadleaf than in coniferous soils. Comammox Nitrospira and AOA were numerically dominant over AOB in all soil samples. The community composition of comammox Nitrospira exhibited significant site-specific characteristics with the northernmost Zhenjiang site distinguished from the two southern sites, and was significantly related to soil p H and mean annual temperature ( p < 0.01), while forest type had a weak and non-significant effect. Alpha diversity was positively associated with soil water content ( p < 0.05). Comammox Nitrospira abundance was negatively correlated with SOC but positively correlated with mean annual precipitation and temperature ( p < 0.01). This study addresses the knowledge gap of comammox Nitrospira in subtropical forests and improves our understanding of niche differentiation among nitrifying microbial communities in forest ecosystems.

### Reviews and syntheses: When does moss act as a methane biofilter? Transport pathways and environmental controls in peatlands

Abstract. Peatlands are critical global carbon stores and the largest natural source of atmospheric methane (CH4). Bryophytes, particularly Sphagnum and submerged brown mosses, can act as effective biological CH4 filters by hosting methanotrophs within their hydrated tissues and providing photosynthetically derived oxygen. In diffusion-dominated systems, moss-associated oxidation can consume 61–99% of upward CH4 flux. However, the presence of moss cover alone does not guarantee ecosystem-scale emission reduction. Synthesizing evidence from 122 studies across field observations, mesocosms, and manipulation experiments, we evaluate the moss biofilter as a realized, state-dependent transport-and-oxidation process. We show that biofilter effectiveness emerges from competition among three distinct pathways: diffusion through the living moss layer, vascular-plant bypass via aerenchyma, and episodic ebullition. Only the diffusive fraction is consistently exposed to the moss oxidation zone. This pathway partitioning is shaped by microtopography and successional trajectories and is highly vulnerable to global-change stressors. Hydrology acts as the master regulator: modest water-table drawdown expands the oxic oxidation layer, whereas severe desiccation damages moss structure and microbial habitats. Furthermore, warming and chronic nitrogen enrichment can destabilize moss–methanotroph metabolic coupling and promote graminoid expansion, exacerbating vascular bypass. For peatland management and paludiculture, hydrological rewetting is necessary but insufficient; without rapid re-establishment of a functional moss layer and mitigation of nutrient legacies, rewetted sites can become bypass-dominated CH4 hotspots. Techniques such as topsoil removal and the moss layer transfer technique (MLTT) can accelerate recovery, provided full CO2–CH4 trade-offs are evaluated. Finally, we highlight priorities for Earth system modeling, emphasizing that models must explicitly separate diffusive, vascular, and ebullitive pathways rather than treating moss cover as a static oxidation parameter.

### Environmental drivers of soil virus diversity across UK habitats

Soil viruses are critical members of the soil microbiome, shaping terrestrial ecosystem processes and microbial dynamics. However, the environmental factors governing their landscape-scale biogeography remain poorly characterised. Here, we analysed bulk soil metagenomic data from the UKCEH Countryside Survey across broad habitat classes to identify the key drivers of viral diversity and community assembly in UK soils. We show that soil pH is the primary predictor of both viral species richness and overall community composition, alongside significant contributions from the co-related soil organic matter and broad habitat type. Viral species diversity exhibited a strongly non-linear relationship with pH, peaking in neutral to slightly acidic soils. Viral community dissimilarity strongly correlated with 16S rRNA bacterial community structure across all taxonomic ranks, with the strongest association at ASV-level resolution. Despite high spatial turnover among viral contigs, and significant habitat-specific enrichment of particular anti-phage defence systems, viral lifestyle proxies like integrase gene frequencies showed no habitat-specific variation. Together, our results demonstrate that UK soil viral communities are primarily structured by soil pH and habitat parameters and closely coupled to patterns of bacterial host distribution, providing a baseline for future investigations and monitoring of change.

### Isolation and Characterization of Peribacillus simplex NVC-PS3 for Reducing Nitrogen Fertilizer in Baby Maize

Excessive nitrogen fertilizer application in baby maize production can contribute to nutrient losses and environmental degradation, highlighting the need for sustainable nutrient management strategies. This study aimed to isolate, characterize, and evaluate the plant growth-promoting potential of an indigenous endophytic bacterium, Peribacillus simplex strain NVC-PS3, for reducing nitrogen fertilizer requirements in baby maize. P. simplex NVC-PS3 was identified based on morphological, physiological, biochemical, and 16S rRNA gene characteristics and evaluated for nitrogenase activity and nitrogen accumulation. Its effects on baby maize growth and yield were subsequently assessed under four urea application rates (350, 263, 175, and 0 kg urea ha−1) using a randomized complete block design with four replications. P. simplex NVC-PS3 exhibited high physiological adaptability, nitrogenase activity, and continuous nitrogen accumulation during cultivation. Seed inoculation improved soil fertility indicators, including soil organic matter, total nitrogen, available phosphorus, exchangeable potassium, and bacterial abundance. Inoculated plants also showed enhanced vegetative growth, chlorophyll content, biomass production, yield components, marketable yield, and nutritional quality. Notably, inoculation combined with a 25% reduction in urea application resulted in agronomic performance that did not differ significantly from that obtained with the full recommended urea rate. This finding suggests the potential of P. simplex NVC-PS3 to partially reduce synthetic nitrogen fertilizer inputs while maintaining crop productivity under the conditions of this experiment. However, further studies involving isotopic N tracing and quantitative assessment of biological nitrogen fixation are required to confirm the contribution of N2 fixation and validate the fertilizer-reduction potential of this strain.
