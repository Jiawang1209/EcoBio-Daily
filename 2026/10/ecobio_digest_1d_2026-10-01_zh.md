### Physicochemical Protection Outweighs Microbial Carbon Use Efficiency in Regulating Soil Organic Carbon Across an Aridity Gradient

ABSTRACT
                  Microbial carbon use efficiency (CUE) has been proposed as a critical determinant of soil organic carbon (SOC) dynamics, yet its relative importance compared with plant carbon inputs, edaphic conditions, microbial attributes, and physicochemical protection remains poorly understood. Here, we conducted large‐scale soil samplings across a 3000‐km aridity gradient on the Tibetan Plateau to evaluate the contribution of microbial CUE to SOC variation. Microbial CUE increased with increasing aridity, whereas SOC declined, resulting in a weak negative relationship between CUE and SOC along the aridity gradient. This increase in CUE was primarily driven by reduced microbial respiration, while microbial growth remained largely unchanged. By contrast, correlation analyses revealed strong positive relationships between SOC and physicochemical protection, plant diversity and carbon inputs, edaphic properties, and microbial attributes. Random forest and structural equation models further showed that SOC variation was more strongly associated with indicators of physicochemical protection, including mineral‐associated organic carbon, clay content, calcium‐bound carbon, and microbial residue carbon than with microbial CUE. Together, our findings challenge the view that microbial CUE alone governs SOC accumulation and highlight the importance of stabilization processes in regulating SOC variations across the aridity gradient.

### Data and code for: Investigating the potential for N2O-reducing rhizobia inoculants in UK agroecosystems

This record contains the data and analysis code supporting the article "Investigating the potential for N₂O-reducing rhizobia inoculants in UK agroecosystems". Rhizobia were isolated from red clover (Trifolium pratense) grown in soils from five land uses: control arable, one- and five-year herbal leys, conventional grassland and permanent grassland. The isolates' whole genomes were sequenced, assembled, classified with GTDB-Tk (GTDB release 232) and screened for denitrification genes. Soil physicochemical properties, microbial biomass, microbial respiration and potential N₂O fluxes were measured for the same soils and compared across land uses. Genome_Analysis_for_Submission.zip contains the HPC pipeline scripts for read processing, genome assembly, quality control, taxonomic classification, annotation and BLAST-based gene searches, together with the reference query sequences used. Soil_Property_Analysis_for_Submission.zip contains the soil and N₂O data and the R scripts for the statistical analyses, figures and tables. The raw reads and genome assemblies are available in NCBI SRA and GenBank under BioProject PRJNA1527459.

### 16S rRNA gene amplicon dataset of soil bacterial communities under three legume species and nitrogen or phosphorus fertilisation

16S rRNA gene (V3–V4) amplicon sequencing of 27 rhizosphere soil samples from a greenhouse pot experiment established on 24 October 2017 in Troubsko, Czech Republic (49.17357 N, 16.50691 E). Three forage legumes: crimson clover (Trifolium incarnatum), white clover (Trifolium repens) and black medick (Medicago lupulina) — were grown for 178 days under three fertilisation regimes (nitrogen, phosphorus, unfertilised control) in three replicates, one pot per sample. Rhizosphere soil was sampled on 20 April 2018. DNA was extracted with the QIAamp PowerFecal DNA Kit (Qiagen); the V3–V4 region was amplified with primers 341F/806R and sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 (250 bp paired-end) by Novogene Co., Ltd. Contents (single archive, 71 files, 1.41 GB): raw_reads/ – demultiplexed raw paired-end reads, 54 gzip-compressed FASTQ files (two per sample), with in-line barcode and primer retained; otu/ – OTU table at 97 % identity (7,998 OTUs) in non-rarefied and rarefied (122,454 tags per sample) form, OTU representative sequences (FASTA), taxonomic assignments against SILVA release 132, and a phylogenetic tree of the representatives (Newick); diversity/ – per-sample alpha-diversity indices; qc/ – per-sample sequencing quality statistics and DNA extraction QC; chemistry/ – soil chemistry of the same 27 pots at two sampling times and of the soil used to fill the pots; sample_metadata.csv – links every sample and read file to treatment, plant species, replicate and barcode pair; data_dictionary.csv – defines every column of every table; README.txt, LICENCE.txt, CHECKSUMS.md5. Sample codes are : C control, N nitrogen, P phosphorus; I T. incarnatum, P T. repens, T M. lupulina; replicate 1–3. The soil chemistry in chemistry/ was first published in Kintl et al. (2020), SGEM 20, 533–540, https://doi.org/10.5593/sgem2020/3.1/s13.069; please cite that article when using it. The dataset is described in an accompanying data article in Data in Brief (submitted).

### Assessment of Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungal Diversity Associated with Onion (Allium cepa L.) in Parner Tehsil, Ahilyanagar District. (Maharashtra).

Abstract In the twenty-first century, the agriculture industry faces several critical challenges, including the need to enhance crop immunity, reduce the dependence on chemical fertilizers, synthetic pesticides, and herbicides, and most importantly, improve overall crop yield and quality. Onion is one of the significant crops that is widely cultivated in Maharashtra. Maharashtra produces around 8.6 million tons of onions annually. Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF) are among the main components of the soil microbiota, with the potential not only to assist in the establishment of the host plant but also to improve overall plant growth. In the present study, we have isolated and identified the diverse species of arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi associated with different villages in the Parner tehsil located. Rhizosphere soil samples collected from four different sites at the study location were processed for isolation of arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi, which were then identified using microscopic examination of morphological features. The AM fungi observed in the soil samples included eight species of Glomus, three species of Acaulospora, and one species of Scutellospora. The most common and dominant mycorrhizal fungi Glomus fasciculatum and Glomus geosporum. The present study was conducted during the period from October 2025 to June 2026. The winter season is characterized by moderate daytime temperatures, distinctly cooler nights, and low humidity.

### Soil viral response to drought disturbance and ecosystem recovery in an artificial rainforest

Anthropogenic climate change is driving increased temperature and altered precipitation patterns in tropical rainforests, yet the impact of severe drought on soil viral communities and their recovery capacity remains poorly understood. Soil viruses can exert strong controls on bacterial populations impacting microbially-mediated soil processes such as carbon cycling. At the same time, viruses are very sensitive to changes in the soil environment. We leverage meta-omics data from soils collected during a two-month drought and one-month recovery experiment carried in the Biosphere 2 Tropical Rainforest artificial biome. Our results indicate that soil viral community structure responds to small scale soil habitat heterogeneity more strongly than on overall changes in moisture. Additionally, the abundance of soil viruses was associated with a higher diversity of metabolites in the soil, suggesting viruses play a key role in soil biogeochemical processes. Altogether, our study suggests that soil viruses are an active part of tropical rainforest soil microbial communities and that is necessary to understand their response to drought.

### Silent Alarms? Unveiling Destabilization Risk in Terrestrial Ecosystem Carbon Fluxes

ABSTRACT
                  Ecosystems are increasingly exposed to the pressures of global change, with climate emerging as one of the most influential drivers. However, the mechanisms leading to increased ecosystem destabilization risk remain poorly understood. In this study, we used eddy‐covariance carbon flux measurements (GPP, NEP, and Re) from 65 terrestrial ecosystems across the globe, including forests, shrublands, and grasslands from boreal, temperate and Mediterranean biomes to investigate early warning signals of destabilization by analyzing indicators of critical slowing down (trends in lag‐1 temporal autocorrelation and interannual variability) and flickering (trends in skewness and kurtosis). We also examined the influence of climate, nutrients, biodiversity, mycorrhizal associations, and vegetation type as potential drivers of these early warning signals. Our results show no widespread transition toward instability, yet a significant subset of sites displayed rising destabilization risk driven mostly by climate variability. Plant biodiversity failed to buffer against this increasing destabilization risk, whereas dominance by either arbuscular or ectomycorrhizal associations appeared to support resilience in NEP. Importantly, we observed that NEP destabilization correlates with reduced carbon sequestration, highlighting a critical feedback mechanism for the global carbon cycle. This research highlights the urgent need to understand the drivers of the temporal behavior of land C fluxes beyond their annual sums in order to properly assess future carbon‐climate feedbacks.
