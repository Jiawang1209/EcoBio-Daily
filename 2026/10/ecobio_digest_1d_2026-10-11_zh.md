### Reconstruction of soil microbiome and elemental cycling by a synthetic microbial community: A sustainable strategy for improving paddy soil fertility and rice production under agrochemical reduction

Intensive rice cultivation often relies on substantial agrochemical inputs to maintain soil fertility and crop productivity, creating challenges for sustainable paddy management. In the present study, a field trial combined with metagenomic sequencing was performed to uncover the responses of rice, paddy soil, and the soil microbiome to a synthetic microbial community (SynCom) containing some photosynthetic bacteria and Bacillus species under a 50% reduction in agrochemical inputs. Agronomic detection showed SynCom increased rice yield by 3.4%, elevated polished rice rate and grain protein, and drastically reduced the yellow rice and lodging rate. Continuous soil monitoring demonstrated obvious increases in soil organic matter, available nitrogen, and total phosphorus across the whole growing period. Metagenomic analyses further illustrated that SynCom restructured soil microbiota, enriched nutrient-metabolizing taxa including Nitrospirota, Chloroflexota, and Anaeromyxobacter, and might have strengthened positive interspecific cooperation of microbes. Additionally, the increased abundance of some functional pathways associated with C/N/S/P mineralization potentially promoted soil nutrient activation. This study reveals the multi-level regulatory pathway of SynCom and supplies theoretical support for green, chemical-saving sustainable rice production.

### Soil Phosphorus Conditions Drives Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungal Community Differentiation and Modulate the Growth of Cenchrus pauciflorus

Cenchrus pauciflorus is a major invasive plant in the sandy lands of northern China, with the Horqin Sandy Land being a hotspot of its invasion, where soil phosphorus availability may influence its spread through arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF). To elucidate the effects of soil phosphorus on AMF communities and their role in the invasion of C. pauciflorus, we conducted a field survey at two sites with contrasting phosphorus backgrounds: Chajintai Ranch (high phosphorus) and Bahuta Sumu (low phosphorus). Rhizosphere soil and root samples of C. pauciflorus and the co-occurring native grass Setaria viridis were collected for high-throughput sequencing of 18S SSU rRNA gene to analyze AMF community composition and diversity. Two AMF species, Funneliformis mosseae and Diversispora epigaea, were selected for a pot experiment with C. pauciflorus. Five phosphorus treatments were established under high- and low-phosphorus conditions using different phosphorus forms. The effects of the two AMF species on the growth and reproduction of C. pauciflorus under different phosphorus treatments were analyzed. The results showed that Glomeraceae and Glomus were the predominant family and genus, respectively. Under Bahuta Sumu, Diversispora was relatively enriched in the roots of C. pauciflorus compared with S. viridis roots. However, other nutrient factors also varied between the two field sites, the independent effect of phosphorus on AMF community composition and diversity could not be fully disentangled. The pot experiment revealed that AMF inoculation did not significantly promote the growth of C. pauciflorus under high-phosphorus treatment. In contrast, when phosphorus was low and supplied in insoluble forms, both AMF species significantly promoted the growth and reproduction of C. pauciflorus. D. epigaea exhibited a stronger growth-promoting effect than F. mosseae, with significantly higher colonization rates in most treatments. In summary, C. pauciflorus is associated with the enrichment of specific AMF taxa in low-phosphorus habitats, which may in turn promote its growth by enhancing the acquisition of insoluble phosphorus. This may represent an important belowground symbiotic mechanism underlying its successful invasion in nutrient-poor sandy habitats.

### A GacS-mediated regulatory framework coordinates bacterial competition and rhizosphere microbiome remodeling in Pseudomonas fluorescens

Plant growth-promoting Pseudomonas bacteria are promising biocontrol agents whose regulatory and effector genes shape bacterial traits involved in interactions with host plants and soil microorganisms. However, previous research has predominantly examined individual genes or traits in isolation, often making it difficult to compare their relative contributions within a single strain. This reductionist approach limits our understanding of how beneficial bacteria simultaneously engage in interactions with plants, suppress pathogens, and shape microbial communities. To address this gap, we selected 16 representative genes and gene clusters in P. fluorescens 2P24 based on genome annotation and previous knowledge of three PGPR-relevant functional layers: protein secretion systems, regulatory pathways, and secondary metabolite biosynthesis. This panel included the major annotated protein secretion systems in strain 2P24, selected regulatory systems with known or predicted roles in bacterial physiology and ecological interactions, and secondary-metabolite-related genes/gene clusters such as phl (2,4-DAPG biosynthetic gene cluster). We then examined how deletion of these genes or gene clusters affected bacterial traits, plant immune elicitation, and soil or rhizosphere bacterial community composition. Through comprehensive genetic and phenotypic analyses, we identified six genes and gene clusters— lapBCE , gacS , phoP , rsmX , fliC , and phl —that are essential for fundamental bacterial physiological processes and ecological fitness. More importantly, we discovered two regulatory modules, GacS-Phl and GacS-FliC, that coordinately govern multi-interface ecological interactions in the rhizosphere. Specifically, GacS regulates the production of 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol, an antimicrobial compound that directly suppresses competing soil-dwelling Streptomyces populations. Simultaneously, GacS modulates flagellin expression, and flagellin acts as a microbe-associated molecular pattern triggering plant immune responses that indirectly reshape the composition and structure of rhizosphere microbiome communities. This study establishes a coordinated regulatory model that integrates global regulatory networks, microbe-associated molecular patterns, and antimicrobial secondary metabolites in mediating complex ecological interactions. Our findings reveal that beneficial Pseudomonas strains employ a GacS-mediated regulatory framework to simultaneously manage multiple ecological interfaces, providing mechanistic insights into how these bacteria balance plant-beneficial activities with competitive strategies. This regulatory framework offers a theoretical foundation for rational design and application of microbial biocontrol agents for sustainable agricultural practices. Video Abstract

### Distinct Strategies for Engineering Bioenergy Traits in Sorghum Drive Divergent Functional Shifts in the Rhizosphere Microbiome

Engineering bioenergy crops for enhanced biomass digestibility and high-value co-products is a promising approach to sustainable biofuel production. However, the impact of these engineered crops on the belowground microbiome remains poorly understood. In this study, we characterized the composition and functional responses of the belowground microbiome associated with transgenic sorghum lines: one with reduced cell wall crosslinking (AT10) and another with elevated protocatechuic acid (PCA) production (Qsub). The lines were grown under field conditions with four irrigation levels, and samples were collected twice during the growing season. We found that the two transgenic types drove a subtle but significant divergence in bacterial communities, both compositionally and functionally. Community-wide functional profiling showed that AT10 rhizospheres were consistently depleted in genes associated with mobile genetic elements, while Qsub rhizospheres were enriched in TonB-dependent transport and vanillate monooxygenase, an enzyme that funnels lignin-derived monomers toward PCA degradation, alongside reduced pectin- and cellulose-degradation genes. Genome-resolved analysis linked these community-level shifts to specific taxa: in Qsub, the enriched MAGs were taxonomically diverse, ranging from genome-reduced Patescibacteria with an episymbiotic lifestyle to taxa enriched in lignin-associated auxiliary activity enzymes. In AT10, the depleted Azospirillaceae MAGs carried unusually high mobile genetic element content, consistent with the community-wide pattern. Collectively, these findings offer new insights into how genetic modifications aimed at improving bioenergy traits shapes the composition and function of the rhizosphere microbiome, with potential downstream consequences for soil carbon cycling.

### Long-term peanut cultivation alters soil organic C stocks and aggregate stability: insights from coupled iron oxide, microbial, and lignin phenol analyses

Introduction The microbial and mineral C pumps play crucial roles in soil organic C (SOC) stabilization via complicated organo-mineral interactions. Yet, how both of them regulate SOC dynamics during peanut cultivation remains elusive. Methods The surface soils from five peanut fields of varying ages (0-, 2-, 5-, 10-, and 20-yr.) were selected to elucidate the temporal variations in SOC dynamics and its potential mineral-microbial synergistic regulation patterns, based on PLFAs, eco-enzymatic activity, amino sugars, lignin phenols and Fe oxides analysis, etc. Results With increasing peanut cultivation ages, SOC, labile C pools, microbial biomass, hydrolase activities, plant‑ and microbial‑derived C (PC and MRC), all exhibited unimodal patterns, peaking at 2-yr. peanut field before subsequently declining. Furthermore, the decreased Cy/Pre (i.e., the ratio of cyclopropyl PLFAs to their monoenoic precursor) and elevated fungi/bacteria values in older peanut fields indicated intensified bacterial physiological stress and a community shift favoring fungal dominance in these fields. Long-term peanut cultivation (i.e., 10 and 20-yr.) decreased SOC lability, enhanced aggregate stability, and altered SOC origins (i.e., enlarged PC but reduced MRC contribution to SOC). As peanut cultivation ages increased, the aggravated soil acidification (evidenced by a decline in pH from 6.10 to 4.57) concurrently enhanced the activation and complexation of Fe oxides, which was attributed to elevated levels of reactive Fe oxides (i.e., Feo and Fep). This transition altered the Fe–C coupling modes from ‘weak’ adsorption-driven (C:Fe molar ratios < 1) in 0- and 2-yr. peanut fields, to ‘strong’ complexation (1 to 6) in the, 5-, 10-, and 20-yr. peanut fields. The PLS-PM and Mantel test further suggested that intensified soil acidification promote reactive Fe oxides accumulation, while labile C pools and microbial biomass positively affect the accumulation of PC and MRC, respectively. Notably, reactive Fe oxides play a significant role only in MRC, rather than in PC accumulation. Discussion Overall, these results elucidate the coupled mineral‑microbial influences on SOC dynamics over the peanut cultivation chronosequence, thereby enhancing our understanding of SOC sequestration processes in China’s peanut-producing regions.

### Modeling the Impact of Contamination on Microbial-Enzyme Dynamics and Soil Function

Globally, contaminated soils pose serious risks to agriculture, biodiversity of ecosystems, and human health. Although a variety of monitoring and remediation methods have been developed over the years, there remains a lack of theoretical modelling to better understand the impact of contamination on soil carbon in a microbially-mediated system. In this study, we develop a model which incorporates contaminated soil organic carbon, uncontaminated soil organic carbon, dissolved organic carbon, microbes, extracellular enzymes, and CO2, to explore how different amounts of contamination and modes of contamination (pulse and press) affect microbially-mediated carbon flux within both vegetated and barren environments. The model represents contamination as a pool of contaminated soil organic carbon that is more resistant to microbial degradation than uncontaminated soil organic carbon. The results show that, in barren environments, the amount and type of contamination has no notable impact on the soil system. In contrast to this, in vegetated environments, plants mitigate the impact of large contamination inputs with differences in outcome depending on the amount and type of contamination.

### Comparative profiling of rhizosphere bacterial communities in male and female date palms reveals sex-associated taxonomic shifts using 16S rRNA amplicon sequencing

Background and aims The rhizosphere microbiome plays a vital role in plant nutrition, health, and resilience, supporting sustainable agriculture and contributing to SDG 2 (Zero Hunger) and SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production). However, comparative studies of rhizosphere bacterial communities associated with male and female date palm ( Phoenix dactylifera L.) plants remain relatively limited. Therefore, this study aimed to compare the rhizosphere bacterial communities associated with male and female date palm plants. Methods Rhizosphere bacterial communities associated with male and female date palm plants were investigated using 16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing to evaluate differences in bacterial community composition and diversity associated with plant sex. Results Across both sexes, the rhizosphere microbiome was dominated by Firmicutes, Proteobacteria, Actinobacteriota, Acidobacteriota, and Chloroflexi, with Bacillus -related taxa prominent at lower taxonomic levels. Shannon diversity and observed features did not differ significantly between female and male samples ( p = 0.1266 and p = 0.13, respectively). In contrast, beta diversity analysis revealed no significant separation between male and female communities, indicating similar overall community structure. Although exploratory differences in relative abundance were observed for certain taxa (Bdellovibrionota, Entotheonellaeota, Planctomycetota, Micromonosporales, Thermomicrobiales, Pedosphaerales, and Chitinophagales). Conclusion Male and female date palm plants harbor broadly similar rhizosphere bacterial communities with analyzing differences in selected taxa. These preliminary observations warrant further investigation with larger sample sizes, functional approaches, and explicit consideration of reproductive stage effects, with potential implications for developing microbiome-informed strategies to enhance soil health and sustainable date palm productivity.
