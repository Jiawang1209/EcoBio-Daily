## 农田土壤微生物

### Genomic plasticity, stress tolerance, and a distinctive pSymA in the symbiotic bacterium Sinorhizobium meliloti IRAMC:0087

Arid ecosystems promote the emergence of rhizobial strains adapted to severe abiotic stress while retaining symbiotic competence. Here, we characterize IRAMC:0087, a rhizobial strain isolated from root nodules of the Saharan shrub Calobota saharae in southern Tunisia. Phenotypic assays showed high tolerance to heat, salinity, and osmotic stress, together with plant growth-promoting traits including indole-3-acetic acid production, phosphate solubilization, and siderophore secretion. In vivo assays demonstrated effective nitrogen-fixing nodulation of Vachellia tortilis , whereas Medicago and Trifolium species were not nodulated despite growth promotion through non-symbiotic mechanisms. Confocal microscopy confirmed root colonization and nodule occupancy, indicating host-dependent infection behavior. To better understand the physiological behavior of this strain, its complete genome sequence was generated, revealing a 7.37 Mb circularized genome comprising five replicons: a 3.65 Mb chromosome, two megaplasmids corresponding to pSymA (0.59 Mb) and pSymB (1.67 Mb), and two accessory plasmids, with an overall GC content of 62%. Phylogenomic, Average Nucleotide Identity (ANI), and digital DNA–DNA hybridization (dDDH) analyses assigned IRAMC:0087 to Sinorhizobium meliloti . However, comparative genomics revealed marked structural and functional divergence of pSymA_IRAMC:0087 from S. meliloti 1,021, with greater similarity to S. garamanticum and S. numidicum . NodC phylogeny placed the strain within symbiovar tunisiaense , consistent with its distinct host range. Genome annotation further identified determinants linked to stress tolerance, rhizosphere competitiveness, bioactive metabolite production, signaling, and symbiosis, including genes located on accessory plasmids. These findings highlight IRAMC:0087 as a desert-adapted rhizobium with distinctive symbiotic properties and strong ecological fitness, providing insights into rhizobial evolution in arid environments.

### Environmental determinants of phenazine- and 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol-producing Pseudomonas in the wheat rhizosphere across a precipitation gradient

ABSTRACT The Columbia Plateau of the Inland Pacific Northwest (IPNW) encompasses one of North America’s largest dryland wheat production regions, where soil moisture strongly influences plant growth, health, and rhizosphere microbial communities. Two ecologically and genetically distinct groups of the Pseudomonas fluorescens complex naturally suppress soilborne wheat pathogens in IPNW fields by producing either phenazine-1-carboxylic acid (Phz+) or 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol (Phl+). To determine how precipitation and associated soil factors shape the abundance of these bacteria, we analyzed wheat root samples from commercial fields spanning a precipitation gradient across the Columbia Plateau. We quantified pairwise relationships among environmental variables using Spearman correlations and constructed a correlation network to visualize patterns of association. Network analysis revealed that precipitation influences soil properties indirectly, with organic matter, clay content, cation exchange capacity, and pH forming an interconnected cluster of correlated variables. Wheat rhizosphere colonization frequency by Phz+ pseudomonads was primarily associated with soil pH, which correlated with precipitation through its effects on weathering and organic matter accumulation, whereas colonization frequency of Phl+ pseudomonads showed weaker associations with abiotic variables. Phz+ pseudomonads were favored in neutral to alkaline, coarse soils typical of the low-precipitation zone, whereas Phl+ populations thrived in wetter, organic-rich soils with lower pH. These findings highlight soil pH as a key environmental filter mediating precipitation-driven differences in Pseudomonas populations and suggest that biotic interactions and plant selection further modulate root colonization. Understanding how these agriculturally important microbial functional groups respond to moisture regimes provides insight into the sustainability of disease-suppressive microbiomes in the IPNW. IMPORTANCE Dryland wheat-based production systems of the Inland Pacific Northwest are highly vulnerable to changing precipitation patterns that affect crop productivity, soilborne pathogens, and soil microbial ecology. This study reveals how long-term rainfall gradients influence the balance between two groups of beneficial Pseudomonas that protect wheat from root diseases. We show that soil pH, closely linked to rainfall, organic matter, and mineral weathering, is a key abiotic factor controlling the presence of phenazine-producing bacteria in dryland wheat grown in low-precipitation zone soils. In contrast, 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol producers dominate in wetter, organic-rich environments. These insights help explain how beneficial microbiomes adapt to environmental constraints and identify soil conditions that promote natural disease suppression. Understanding these mechanisms will aid in predicting microbiome resilience and guiding management strategies to maintain wheat productivity under changing weather conditions.

### The combination of microbial agent, organic fertilizer, and silica powder enhances oat yield and quality through regulating rhizosphere processes

Aims Soil salinization is a global challenge for sustainable agricultural development. Composite modifiers have the potential to boost the agricultural productivity of salinized soils. However, it’s still unclear whether it outperforms single modifiers in regulating rhizosphere soil properties, microbial communities, and forage yield and quality of different oat varieties ( Avena sativa L.) and the mechanisms. Methods In this field experiment in 2023–2024 in the arid western China, five modifier treatments, namely CK (control group without any modifier), TJ (microbial agent), TF (organic fertilizer), TG (silica powder), and TJFG (microbial agent + organic fertilizer + silica powder), were designed for two oat varieties (TY 60, Tianyan 60; MST 2, Musite 2). Then, their effects on salinized soil properties, rhizosphere microbial communities, and the interaction between soil nutrient cycling and microbial composition were explored. Results The crude protein (CP) content of the two oat varieties peaked under the TJFG treatment. The rhizosphere soil NH 4 + -N, available phosphorus (AP), available potassium (AK), organic matter (SOM), and water content (SWC) of the TJFG treatment were significantly higher than those of CK, while the soil pH and NO 3 − -N content were significantly lower. In addition, the AP content of the TJFG treatment was 70.17, 44.91, and 66.93% (average over 2 years) higher than that of the TJ, TF, and TG treatments, respectively. Conclusion The application of TJFG improved the diversity of bacterial and fungal communities as well as the activities of phosphatase (ATP), urease (UE), and catalase (CAT), thereby promoting soil nutrient transformations and enhancing soil fertility, oat yield, and quality. Chloroflexota and Ascomycota were the key microbial phyla to regulate oat yield. Organic matter, AK, AP, and CAT activity were the main soil factors affecting yield. Overall, MST 2 has greater growth potential and more value for promotion and application than TY 60. This study provides an important reference for salinized land use, ecological health maintenance, and sustainable agricultural production in arid regions.

### Row Ratio in Faba Bean–Oat Intercropping Enhances System Productivity by Regulating Rhizosphere Bacterial Communities and Root Traits in the Qinghai Lake Region

The biological mechanisms through which faba bean–oat intercropping coordinates interspecific competition, belowground responses, and system productivity remain poorly understood in alpine environments. A field experiment was conducted in the alpine region surrounding Qinghai Lake, with six treatments comprising faba bean monoculture (F), oat monoculture (O), and four faba bean–oat intercropping patterns with row ratios of 1:1 (FO), 2:1 (F2O), 2:2 (F2O2), and 1:2 (FO2). Agronomic traits, root morphology, soil physicochemical properties, and rhizosphere bacterial communities were measured to clarify above- and below-ground coordinated responses of the two crops to varying row ratios. The 2:1 faba bean–oat intercropping yielded the optimal performance, with a land equivalent ratio of 1.479 and a 29.3% higher dry matter yield (6795.4 kg ha−1) than faba bean monoculture, as well as the highest system crude protein production. Dominant oat compensatory growth primarily boosted system productivity. Compared with the corresponding monocultures, the 2:1 intercropping treatment increased the total root length, root surface area, and root tip number of faba bean by 23.9%, 33.9%, and 54.3%, respectively, while increasing the total root length of oat by 110.3%. Under this treatment, soil alkali-hydrolyzable nitrogen reached 167.17 mg kg−1, 39.9% higher than that under oat monoculture, whereas soil available phosphorus and catalase activity increased by 13.2% and 54.4%, respectively. Rhizosphere bacterial communities exhibited host-specific responses, with stronger treatment-associated differentiation in the oat rhizosphere and enrichment of Bacteroidia, Chitinophagales, and Chitinophagaceae under F2O. Partial least squares path modeling (PLS-PM) further revealed crop-specific pathways linking row configuration, belowground properties, bacterial α-diversity, and crop performance. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that the productivity advantage of faba bean–oat intercropping at a 2:1 row ratio is jointly mediated by competitive compensation of oat, enhanced root proliferation, improved soil nitrogen and phosphorus availability, and restructuring of rhizosphere bacterial communities.

### Advancing Cadmium Remediation via Rhizosphere Engineering: From Microbial Mechanisms to Field Deployment

Cadmium (Cd) contamination of agricultural soils threatens food security and ecosystem health worldwide, yet conventional cleanup methods remain costly and ecologically damaging. Phytoremediation offers a sustainable in situ alternative, but low metal bioavailability, insufficient plant biomass, and inconsistent microbial performance limit field deployment. This review provides a framework that links molecular plant–microbe interactions to practical rhizosphere engineering for Cd management. We compare how functional traits of plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR), arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF), and endophytes influence Cd mobilization versus immobilization, pH tolerance, and persistence. Their functions overlap and depend on plant species, Cd concentration, soil properties, and microbial strain; nevertheless, the evidence points to general tendencies: PGPR often drive short-term Cd mobilization with limited persistence, AMF commonly promote more durable immobilization through hyphal sequestration, and endophytes frequently modulate internal metal transport. We also evaluate synthetic consortia, multi-omics-guided microbiome design, and soil amendments. Field outcomes are appraised against explicit criteria (trial duration, replication across sites, use of historically contaminated soils, and reporting of Cd removal rate, translocation factor, available soil Cd, biomass, and soil health indicators), which also underpin the proposed standardized validation protocols. Matching microbial traits to site-specific goals, alongside food-safety, cost, and regulatory considerations, can help move phytoremediation toward scalable deployment work provides environmental managers and policymakers with a comprehensive roadmap for sustainable Cd remediation in agricultural landscapes.

### Soil Functional Roles of Trichoderma in Sustainable Wheat Production: Insights from a Global Meta-Analysis and Soil Metagenomics

Soil microbial communities play essential roles in maintaining soil functions, nutrient cycling, and sustainable crop production. Beneficial fungi such as Trichoderma represent promising biological tools for integrated soil management by enhancing plant performance and reducing reliance on chemical inputs. However, the consistency of Trichoderma-mediated benefits across agricultural systems and the functional mechanisms underlying these effects remain unclear. This study integrates a global meta-analysis with soil metagenomic profiling to evaluate the contribution of Trichoderma to sustainable wheat production. Analysis of 293 independent experiments demonstrated that Trichoderma inoculation significantly improved wheat plant height, root length, shoot and root biomass, and grain yield, with the strongest responses observed in root-related traits. Species-specific effects were evident, with T. afroharzianum, T. harzianum, T. asperellum, T. longibrachiatum, and T. viride all showing positive impacts, whereas unidentified Trichoderma spp. did not. Metagenomic analysis of long-term wheat soils from Australia and China identified 45 Trichoderma-assigned genes, mostly related to metabolism, including pathways linked to nutrient acquisition, nitrate reduction, secondary metabolite biosynthesis, and pathogen suppression. These findings demonstrate that Trichoderma contributes to soil functional improvement by promoting nutrient-related processes, strengthening plant–microbe interactions, and supporting wheat productivity. The results highlight the potential of beneficial soil fungi as sustainable components of integrated soil management strategies for improving food production while reducing environmental impacts.

### A Unified Quantitative Framework for Assessing Uncertainty in Microbial-Explicit Soil Carbon Models under Diverse Data Availability of Field Observations

Abstract Microbial-explicit soil carbon models offer mechanistic representations of soil carbon turnover, but their application to the field remains limited, which is commonly attributed to the scarce microbial observations. However, usually there are significant mismatches between model data requirements and the diverse data availability of field observations, which may greatly affect the uncertainty of soil carbon models and have been largely overlooked. Here, we developed a unified framework that systematically quantified how the field observation type, sampling frequency, and measurement uncertainty jointly constrained model parameters and long-term predictions of three representative microbial-explicit soil carbon models. We quantified how well observations of soil carbon stocks, soil respiration, microbial biomass carbon, and functional gene abundance can reduce the parameter and prediction uncertainties across a comprehensive set of field scenarios. High-frequency, low-error soil respiration observations, when combined with soil carbon measurements, dominated uncertainty reduction across carbon pools, while interestingly, microbial observations only provided conditional improvements for microbial and enzyme-related states. More microbial data did not necessarily yield more effective calibration information, and improvements in prediction accuracy required microbial observations with sufficiently small measurement errors. The unified quantitative framework would also help to design a data strategy when developing new soil carbon models to accurately predict soil carbon cycling.

### Measurement report: Soil reactive nitrogen gas emissions in the Tibetan Plateau

Abstract. The Tibetan Plateau, highly sensitive to climate change, exerts strong atmospheric oxidation capacity partly through rapid cycling of atmospheric reactive nitrogen (Nr). Soil Nr emissions play a crucial role in atmospheric nitrogen cycling and oxidation capacity, yet their emissions on the Tibetan Plateau remain poorly quantified. Combining dynamic chamber measurements, laboratory analysis, and a parameterized model, we assessed the characteristics, driving factors, spatial distribution and annual emissions of soil Nr in the Tibetan Plateau. We found that the optimum soil fluxes of nitrous acid (HONO), nitric oxide (NO), nitrogen dioxide (NO2), and ammonia (NH3) were 21.6 ± 8.4, 43.7 ± 14.7, 15.8 ± 1.3, and 190.0 ± 116.3 ng N m−2 s−1, respectively. These emissions were mainly influenced by soil pH, nutrient content, and microbial community composition. After exposure to atmospheric NOx and ozone (O3), Nr emissions from forest soils were enhanced but those from croplands and grasslands were suppressed. The estimated annual emissions of soil HONO, NO, and NOx from Tibetan Plateau to be 7.0 ± 3.4 Gg N yr−1, 11.6 ± 7.8 Gg N yr−1, and 20.3 ± 7.0 Gg N yr−1, respectively. Soil HONO emissions contribute approximately 10.5 % of the external (NOx-independent) daytime atmospheric HONO sources and modulate the regional atmospheric chemical balance by elevating the HONO/NOx ratio. Our results provide the first integrated quantification of soil Nr emissions on the Tibetan Plateau and emphasize their importance for regional nitrogen cycling and atmospheric oxidation capacity.
