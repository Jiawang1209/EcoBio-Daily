### Invasive Chinese tallow is associated with distinct rhizosphere microbial communities, core microbiomes, and functional potential relative to native Loblolly pine

Plant invasions frequently reshape belowground microbial communities, yet their effects on rhizosphere microbiome structure and function remain poorly understood. We compared the rhizosphere bacterial and fungal microbiomes of invasive Chinese tallow (Triadica sebifera) and native Loblolly pine (Pinus taeda) using 16S rRNA gene and ITS amplicon sequencing, together with microbial, functional, and soil analyses. Chinese tallow exhibited significantly greater bacterial and fungal richness and distinct microbial community composition than Loblolly pine. The invasive rhizosphere was enriched in plant-beneficial bacterial taxa, including Streptomyces, Bradyrhizobium, and Rhizobium, and ectomycorrhizal-associated fungi such as Russula and Cenococcum. This enrichment is consistent with enhanced nutrient acquisition, plant growth promotion, and stress tolerance, potentially favoring Chinese tallow establishment and persistence. Further, core microbiome and co-occurrence network analyses revealed extensive reorganization of persistent (Streptomyces, Russula) and highly connected microbial taxa that may be associated with Chinese tallow invasion. Functional predictions indicated greater representation of carbohydrate degradation, nitrogen fixation, plant growth promotion, nutrient mineralization, and ectomycorrhizal guilds in the Chinese tallow rhizosphere, whereas lignin degradation and saprotrophic or pathogen-associated fungal guilds were relatively more abundant under Loblolly pine. Soil physicochemical properties were also strongly correlated with dominant bacterial and fungal taxa, indicating that host-mediated microbial recruitment and environmental filtering jointly shaped rhizosphere community assembly. Overall, our findings identify rhizosphere microbiome reassembly as an important belowground feature associated with Chinese tallow invasion.

### Analysis of fungal diversity and soil metabolomics in rhizosphere of blueberry under multiple acid-heavy metal pollution

Vaccinium spp. grow well in acidic soils, tolerate heavy metals, and rely on mycorrhizae for nutrient uptake. Using ITS sequencing and untargeted metabolomics, this study examined fungal communities in the rhizosphere, root surfaces, endophytes, and soil metabolites under acid and heavy metal stress. Cd addition slightly impacted fungal α-diversity but significantly altered dominant taxa composition. Key genera like Sebacina and Pseudogymnoascus declined, while Oidiodendron, Mycosymbioces, and unclassified Agaricomycetes increased and became dominant. Rare or tolerant species such as Trechispora, Archaeorhizomyces, and Acidomyces also emerged as dominant. Among 364 soil secondary metabolites, 31 changed significantly, especially lipids and lipid-like molecules, which increased and were linked to Sebacina and Archaeorhizomyces shifts.

### Genome sequencing and broad-spectrum plant growth-promoting effects of the multifunctional PGPR strain Priestia aryabhattai B8

ABSTRACT A multifunctional plant growth-promoting bacterial strain designated as B8 was isolated from the rhizosphere soil of tobacco under continuous cropping. According to morphological, biochemical, and physiological characteristics, and supported by genomic phylogenetic analysis, the strain was identified as Priestia aryabhattai . Phenotypic assays showed that B8 exhibits multiple plant growth-promoting traits, including indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) production, phosphate and potassium solubilization, and siderophore production. Whole-genome sequencing and functional annotation revealed that the genome of B8 consists of one circular chromosome and two plasmids, harboring a complete tryptophan biosynthesis pathway ( trpABCDEF ), a phosphate-specific transport system ( pstSABC ), potassium transporter genes ( khtT/khtU ), and a siderophore biosynthetic gene cluster. Additionally, genes encoding carbohydrate-active enzymes, such as chitinases and β-glucanases involved in cell wall degradation, along with multiple secondary metabolite biosynthetic gene clusters, were identified, providing a molecular basis for its multifunctional plant growth-promoting and biocontrol potential. Pot experiments indicated that inoculation with strain B8 was associated with significantly increased contents of IAA, available phosphorus, and available potassium in the rhizosphere soils of tobacco, pak choi, and wheat, as well as enhanced plant growth and root development. Field trials further suggested that B8 treatment correlated with significantly improved tobacco plant height, stem circumference, and maximum leaf area, along with elevated contents of total sugar, reducing sugar, and potassium in tobacco leaves. Multivariate statistical analyses pointed to soil available phosphorus, available potassium, and IAA content as key factors driving crop growth. This study provides insights into the multifunctional mechanisms of Priestia aryabhattai B8, offering a theoretical foundation and microbial resource for biofertilizer development. IMPORTANCE This work advances sustainable agriculture by introducing a multifunctional soil bacterium, Priestia aryabhattai B8, as an eco-friendly alternative to reduce reliance on chemical fertilizers. By demonstrating that this single strain enhances the growth and quality of diverse crops, including tobacco, pak choi, and wheat, in pot experiments and by further validating its growth-promoting effects on tobacco in the field, this research confirms the practical potential of beneficial microbes for improving crop productivity. The comprehensive genomic and functional insights gained into the bacterium’s nutrient-solubilizing and hormone-producing mechanisms provide a solid foundation for designing robust and predictable biofertilizers. Overall, this investigation contributes to lowering chemical inputs, restoring soil fertility, and promoting environmentally sustainable food production systems.

### Microbial inoculants improve salt tolerance of Sesbania cannabina by regulating rhizosphere microbial communities and nutrient cycling genes

Soil salinization constrains plant growth and soil ecological function, microbial inoculation offers a sustainable route for saline-alkali soils. We evaluated how inoculants influence Sesbania cannabina growth and rhizosphere processes under different NaCl levels (0, 150, 300 mM). Seedlings were inoculated with either a synthetic community (Syncom; T1), a single salt-tolerant nitrogen-fixing strain (T2), or left uninoculated (CK). We measured plant growth, antioxidant enzyme activities, soil nutrient pools, rhizosphere microbial community structure, and relative abundance of nitrogen and phosphorus cycling genes. Inoculation significantly promoted plant height and fresh weight ( p < 0.05), and increased AN, TN, and OM, with Syncom (T1) providing the strongest growth response under salt stress (150 mM NaCl). Inoculation also elevated the abundance of nitrogen and phosphorus cycling genes. Path analyses integrating soil properties, microbial communities, functional genes, and plant traits supported a cascading framework in which soil nutrients and phosphorus cycling functional genes directly influence plant defense. Overall, appropriate microbial inoculants (T1) alleviate salt stress in S. cannabina by coordinating soil-microbe-plant interactions, highlighting their potential for remediation of saline-alkaline soils.

### Soil CH4 and CO2 emissions mediated by water and afforestation in Horqin Sandy Land

Ecological restoration in arid and semi-arid regions plays a vital role in maintaining regional carbon balance under global change. Although water management and vegetation composition regulate soil carbon cycling, their combined effects on greenhouse gas source/sink dynamics in sandy ecosystems and the underlying microbial mechanisms remain unclear. This study examined sand dunes (Sd) and 35-year-old typical afforestation types including Caragana microphylla Lam. (Cm), Populus simonii Carr. (Ps), Pinus sylvestris var . mongolica Litv. (Pm), and mixed forests of Ps + Pm and Pm + Cm in the Horqin Sandy Land. Through a controlled incubation experiment at 30% and 60% water holding capacity (WHC), we systematically analyzed the effects of afforestation and water management on soil methane (CH 4 ) and carbon dioxide (CO 2 ) emissions. Linear regression analyses of functional gene abundances and environmental factors further revealed microbial mechanisms. All afforested soils acted as CH 4 sinks, Ps, Pm, Ps + Pm, and Pm + Cm showed the strongest CH 4 uptake, correlated with higher pmoA gene abundance and lower mcrA / pmoA . 30% WHC enhanced CH 4 oxidation by improving soil aeration, whereas CO 2 emissions increased significantly under elevated water content. Cm showed the highest CO 2 emissions, whereas mixed forests (Ps + Pm and Pm + Cm) maintained high CH 4 uptake even under high CO 2 emissions. These findings indicate that water management and afforestation legacy interactively shape carbon cycling pathways in sandy ecosystems. Mixed forests optimize microbial community function, balancing carbon cycling efficiency with greenhouse gas mitigation across water regimes, providing a scientific basis for carbon management in ecological restoration of the Horqin Sandy Land and comparable semi-arid regions.

### Effects of important ecological measures on plant diseases and soil microbial communities in the Hulunber Grassland

Introduction Ecological management measures can influence plant diseases, forage production, soil nutrients, and microbial communities, but their integrated effects in natural meadow grasslands remain unclear. This study evaluated the effects of mowing twice per year, microbial agent application, and organic fertilizer application in the Hulunber natural grassland. Methods Field plots were subjected to mowing twice per year (MT), microbial agent application (MA), and organic fertilizer application (OF), with untreated plots serving as the control (CK). Plant community and disease surveys, soil physicochemical analyses, and high-throughput sequencing were combined to assess treatment effects on plant community characteristics, foliar fungal diseases, soil nutrients, and soil microbial communities. Results MA significantly increased plant community cover, whereas MT significantly reduced community height. MT increased fresh and hay forage yields by 89.26% and 85.62%, respectively, and OF also significantly increased forage yield. Leaf spot was the predominant plant disease, with peak incidence occurring in August. MA and MT significantly reduced community pathogen load, and MT produced the strongest reduction, decreasing community pathogen load by 56.60% relative to CK. At the functional-group level, MA and MT reduced pathogen loads in grasses and sedges, while all treatments reduced pathogen loads in legumes by 47.52%–65.33%. MA increased soil total nitrogen; MT increased total nitrogen, ammonium nitrogen, and soil organic carbon; and OF increased soil organic carbon. OF increased fungal alpha diversity, while bacterial diversity and community composition differed among treatments. MT increased the relative abundance of Fusarium , whereas MA, MT, and OF decreased the relative abundance of Alternaria . Discussion Mowing twice per year and microbial agent application showed greater potential for reducing foliar fungal disease, whereas organic fertilizer mainly improved forage production, soil organic carbon, and fungal diversity. These findings provide an ecological basis for management strategies that balance disease reduction, forage production, and soil microbial conservation in meadow grasslands.

### Microclimate characteristics, temporal dynamics of soil bacterial communities, and potential co-occurrence network differences between greenhouse and open-field cultivation environments

Introduction Greenhouse cultivation modifies agricultural microclimates and may be associated with temporal changes in soil microbial communities. We investigated a representative vegetable greenhouse and an adjacent open-field site in the subtropical red-soil region of Jiangxi Province, China. Methods Air temperature and relative humidity were monitored continuously for one year in both environments, while soil temperature and volumetric water content were monitored within the greenhouse. Monthly soil samples were analyzed using 16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing. Results The greenhouse exhibited pronounced daytime warming, nighttime heat retention, and generally higher relative humidity. Soil bacterial community composition differed significantly between greenhouse and open-field soils, while alpha-diversity differences varied among seasons. Month-resolved analyses revealed significant temporal dynamics. Air temperature and relative humidity jointly explained 15.59% of the variation in bacterial community structure. Potential co-occurrence networks also differed between the two environments in topology and in the proportions of positive and negative correlations. Discussion The results document year-round differences in microclimate and soil bacterial community dynamics between greenhouse and open-field cultivation environments. The measured atmospheric microclimate variables showed statistically detectable but limited associations with bacterial community composition, providing a basis for soil microbiome monitoring and environmental management in subtropical protected agriculture.
