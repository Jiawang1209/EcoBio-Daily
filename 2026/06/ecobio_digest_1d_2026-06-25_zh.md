### Rhizosphere Engineering for Enhancing Nitrogen Mobilization in Plants

Nitrogen is a vital element in plant growth; agricultural systems have considerable inefficiency in use of nitrogen because of over-dependency on chemical-based fertilizers which jeopardize sustainability of the ecosystem. This chapter hypothesizes that rhizosphere engineering, which is a strategic control of microbial community surrounding the root, can be used to improve crop nitrogen mobilization. It explores the exploitation of plant growth-promoting processes and especially with nodule-enhancing rhizobacteria (NER) and synergistic synthetic microbial communities (SynComs) to make processes such as the biological fixation of nitrogen in legumes as efficient as possible. Moreover, this chapter also discusses how the CRISPR/Cas technologies can be transformed to enable accurate microbiome engineering to enhance plant-microbe relationships. Finally, harnessing of the complicated rhizosphere microbiome has proven to be a promising direction toward creating more sustainable and productive agricultural systems that do not rely on synthetic inputs but increase the efficiency with which nutrients are used.

### Microbial Engineering of the Rhizosphere

Rhizospheric microbial engineering depicts a transformative approach focusing on enhanced plant health, resilience, and productivity in Brassica juncea (Indian mustard). This chapter defines a multifaceted functional mechanism by which adventitious microbes support sustainability and plant growth in agroecosystems. Major actions involved are nutrient mobilization by biological nitrogen fixation, phosphate solubilization, and enhanced nutrient uptake. Rhizobacteria and fungi also induce plant immunity by ISR (induced systemic resistance) and SAR (systemic acquired resistance), being a part of crosstalk between phytohormonal pathways. Also, microbes have a critical role in abiotic stress mitigation as drought and heat, by modulating antioxidant responses via production of osmoprotectants and enhancing root system architecture. Their biocontrol potential is also significant, offering antibiosis suppression, competition, and niche exclusion to the pathogens, that is, against soilborne pathogens affecting mustard. Through these diverse functional routes, rhizosphere microbes act as dynamic bio-effectors which improve stress resilience, decrease agrochemical dependence, and promote sustainable cultivation of B. juncea. This chapter depicts the importance of integral microbial mechanisms in crop management frameworks, particularly in the face of escalating climate challenges and soil degradation.

### Rhizosphere Engineering for Abiotic Stress Tolerance in Plants

Rhizosphere engineering is an innovative approach aimed at increasing the capacity of plants to withstand environmental stresses such as heat, humidity, drought, salt, metals such as lead, and nutrient deficits. The active interaction within the rhizosphere essentially determines how plants adjust under stress, which is due to the intricate biochemical relationships between microorganisms, earth, and the source of plants. The engineering of soil management through beneficial microbes and amendments aims to enhance stress resistance in plants. Key contributors like plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria function by producing phytohormones, osmoprotectants, exopolysaccharides, and antioxidant enzymes, which mitigate oxidative stress. Mycorrhizal fungi improve nutrient and water uptake, and root structure. Nano-enhancement techniques enhance root–microbe interactions via genomic profiling, enabling the identification of rhizosphere biomarkers for synthetic microbiome development. Compost and polysaccharide hydrogels enhance soil structure, moisture retention, and microbial communities, contributing to soil health and ecological sustainability. Rhizosphere engineering is a sustainable approach to maintain agricultural productivity amid climate change. Future research should focus on field validation and scalable technologies to improve root–microbe interactions and crop resilience to stressors. This chapter explores the mechanisms and strategies of rhizosphere engineering aimed at enhancing plant tolerance to abiotic stresses, with a focus on plant–soil–microbe interactions, root exudate dynamics, and the application of beneficial microorganisms and advanced biotechnological approaches.

### Foliar metal micronutrients reshape rhizosphere soil multifunctionality by filtering microbial life-history strategies

Foliar application of metal micronutrients is increasingly adopted in intensive cultivation systems, yet its potential ecological risks to rhizosphere functions remain poorly understood. Here, using the medicinal plant Panax notoginseng as a model, we conducted a gradient foliar amendment experiment with iron (Fe), zinc (Zn), and copper (Cu) to evaluate how aboveground metal inputs regulate rhizosphere soil multifunctionality (MF) through microbial life-history strategies. By integrating 16S rRNA amplicon sequencing, metagenomics, root transcriptomics, and a newly developed quantitative Yield-Acquisition-Stress tolerance (qYAS) framework, we disentangled the microbial mechanisms underlying divergent functional responses to metal amendments. Foliar Fe significantly enhanced multifunctionality, including nutrient provision and element cycling, while Cu and Zn reduced nutrient provision and element cycling, but enhanced plant pathogen abundances. These changes were closely associated with shifts in bacterial life-history strategies: Fe promoted Y-strategists characterized by efficient carbon use, streamlined genomes, and high network connectivity, whereas Cu and Zn enriched AS-strategists with larger genomes and negative associations with multifunctionality. Partial least squares path modeling (PLS-PM) further identified microbial strategies as key mediators linking foliar metal inputs, plant performance, soil properties, and multifunctionality. This study provides a trait-based microbial framework for evaluating foliar metal fertilization and guiding safer nutrient management.
