### Mapping the soil microbiome functions shaping wetland methane emissions

ABSTRACT Accounting for only 8% of Earth’s land cover, freshwater wetlands remain the foremost contributors to global methane emissions. Yet the microorganisms and processes underlying methane emissions from wetland soils remain poorly understood. Over a five-year period, we surveyed the microbial membership and in situ methane measurements from over 700 samples in one of the most prolific methane-emitting wetlands in the United States. We constructed a catalog of 2,502 metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs), with more than half of the 70 bacterial and archaeal phyla sampled containing novel lineages. Integration of these data with 133 soil metatranscriptomes provided a genome-resolved view of the biogeochemical specialization and versatility expressed over wetland soil spatial and temporal gradients. Centimeter-scale depth differences best explained patterns of microbial community structure and transcribed functionalities, even more than land cover or temporal information. Moreover, while extended flooding restructured soil redox, this perturbation failed to reconfigure the transcriptional profiles of methane-cycling microorganisms, contrasting with theoretically expected responses to hydrological perturbations. Co-expression analyses, coupled with depth-resolved methane measurements, revealed the metabolisms and trophic structures most predictive of methane hotspots. Mapping the spatiotemporal transcriptional patterns on this compendium of biogeochemically classified soil-derived genomes begins to untangle the microbial carbon, energy, and nutrient processing contributing to wetland methane production. IMPORTANCE Soil microbial ecology is increasingly recognized as essential to climate mitigation, but realizing its full potential requires shifting from static genome inventories to dynamic assessments of microbial activity. This study shows that methane-cycling microbes exhibit stable, depth-stratified expression patterns, even in response to major redox and flooding shifts, undermining assumptions that water-table manipulations common in wetland management can alone reduce methanogenesis. Instead, methane cycling is shaped by spatially organized, transcriptionally active networks involving not only methanogens but also methanotrophs, fermenters, and iron reducers. These findings expose the limitations of genome-only models and highlight the need for soil diagnostics that capture in situ activity. Together, we provide a foundation for developing activity-based microbiome tools, embedding microbial functions into Earth system models, and designing interventions that move beyond “single-lever” strategies and instead work with the structure and dynamics of microbial communities as complex, layered systems.

### Phase-specific responses of soil fungal communities to drought stress in cultivated soils

Soil fungal communities contribute to soil health and plant growth by regulating nutrient cycling, decomposing organic matter, and forming symbiotic associations; however, their stage-specific responses to drought remain unclear. To address this, we investigated the effects of drought stress on cultivated soil fungal communities across three summer maize growth stages the jointing-tasseling stage, tasseling-filling stage, and filling-maturation stage. Specifically, we measured the soil properties (soil water content, ammonium nitrogen , accessible phosphorus, available potassium, organic matter, and soil pH) and evaluated their links to fungal dynamics through structural equation modeling and co-occurrence network analysis. Our results demonstrated that drought stress induced highly stage-specific fungal responses. Although dominant phyla (<i>Ascomycota, Mortierellomycota, and Basidiomycota</i>) exhibited robust drought resistance, fungal co-occurrence networks were profoundly restructured. Drought reduced network complexity during the early-to-mid stages, whereas it triggered a massive topological expansion and a drastic loss of stability during the filling-maturity stage. Furthermore, environmental driving mechanisms shifted from a direct moisture-nitrogen coupled constraint in the early stage to an indirect, phosphorus-dominated regime in the late stage. Ultimately, these findings highlight the dynamic ecological adaptations and specific vulnerabilities of the plant-soil microbiome under prolonged drought, providing essential insights for agricultural and ecological management.

### Long-read metagenomics reveals microbial taxonomic and functional disruptions after fumigation in Florida potato soils

Soil fumigation and phosphorus (P) fertilization are widely used in potato production systems to secure yield, yet their combined effects on soil microbial function and nutrient-use efficiency remain poorly understood. We conducted a field experiment integrating long-read metagenomics with agronomic measurements across four key time points, from pre-fumigation to tuberization, under five P fertilizer rates in sandy soils of Northeast Florida. Fumigation with 1,3-dichloropropene was associated with significant shifts in bacterial and fungal communities. Those shifts were followed by an incomplete recovery observed 62 days after fumigation. Fumigation was also associated with an increase in bacterial alpha diversity, primarily by suppressing dominant, ecologically important taxa such as Bradyrhizobium . Metagenomic analysis showed that fumigation was significantly associated with depletion of gene sets involved in nitrogen acquisition, colonization, competitive exclusion, and phytohormone production, and enrichment of genes associated with plant cell wall degradation, suggesting a shift in plant–microbe interactions. Genes used as indicators of soil health also shifted, with arylsulfatase declining and β-glucosidase increasing, reflecting altered nutrient-cycling potential. Despite increasing soil P availability, higher P fertilization did not enhance yield, P uptake, or P use efficiency, suggesting that fumigation may be associated with disruption of microbial nutrient-cycling functions affecting the crop's ability to convert fertilizer inputs into productive growth. The yield response showed diminishing returns at higher P rates. Overall, our results suggest that fumigation was associated with shifts in soil microbial communities and in the biological pathways underlying fertilizer efficiency, highlighting a trade-off between pest control and sustainable nutrient management in intensive potato systems.

## 草地土壤微生物

### Prairie exudate-microbiome in vitro interactions

Collection of datasets characterizing interactions between plant root exudates and soil microbial communities from tallgrass prairie species under controlled in vitro conditions. The experiment consisted of incubation of root exudates in soil-mimicking liquid medium with prairie soil microbiome in deep-well 24 well plate. The resulting microbiome and metabolome was analyzed. This experiment is a part of the PRISM project. The collection contains a file "MZmine-batch-params" which encloses batch parameter files to re-create MZmine workflow for metabolomics. Another folder "16S-seq_Zymo" contains results of microbiome composition analysis (Full-length 16S rRNA sequencing) conducted by Zymo Research. We also provide metadata sample overview table in .xlsx format with final OD600 values and naming conventions to connect sample names between -omics analyses.
