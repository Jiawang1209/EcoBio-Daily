### Rhizosphere-driven redistribution of denitrification, anammox, and Feammox contributions to nitrogen loss in paddy soil

This dataset contains selected supporting data for the study “Rhizosphere-driven redistribution of denitrification, anammox, and Feammox contributions to nitrogen loss in paddy soil.” It includes processed 16S rRNA gene amplicon sequence variant (ASV) abundance data for bacterial taxa potentially associated with denitrification, anammox, and Feammox, as presented in Figure 4, together with soil physicochemical data covering pH, NH₄⁺, NO₃⁻, Fe(II), Fe(III), total carbon (TC), and total nitrogen (TN). The experiment comprised three nitrogen fertilization levels (N0, 0 mg N kg⁻¹ soil; N1, 100 mg N kg⁻¹ soil; and N2, 300 mg N kg⁻¹ soil), with six independent replicates per treatment (18 pots total). Rhizosphere and non-rhizosphere soils were collected separately after 40 days of rice growth, yielding 36 paired soil samples. The ASV data were generated from bacterial 16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing, processed using QIIME 2 and DADA2, and taxonomically classified against the SILVA 138 database. Representative bacterial taxa were grouped according to their putative associations with denitrification, anammox, and Feammox based on published literature. Their relative abundances were used to characterize microbial responses to nitrogen addition across soil compartments, with the unfertilized N0 treatment serving as the reference for Figure 4. This archive provides selected processed microbial abundance and environmental data supporting the interpretation of nitrogen-cycling microbial communities and their relationships with soil physicochemical conditions. Taxonomic associations with nitrogen transformation pathways are putative and do not independently establish functional activity. Raw sequencing reads, complete laboratory measurements, and other experimental datasets are not included in this deposit. The raw 16S rRNA gene sequencing data are available separately through the NCBI Sequence Read Archive under BioProject accession PRJNA1508300. This is a selected supporting-data deposit rather than a complete raw-data or reproducibility archive.

### Northern palsa and peat ecosystem microbiomes are potentially supported by hydrogen and carbon monoxide metabolism

Northern peatland and permafrost ecosystems are facing rapid changes, with increasing temperatures, permafrost thaw and shifts in hydrology. Characterizing microbial communities and metabolic potential from the soils and associated water bodies is crucial to understanding the changes in biogeochemical cycling in these ecosystems. For this, samples were collected and sequenced from subarctic palsa and peat soils, along with samples from associated thaw ponds and streams. Metagenomics was utilized in describing the microbiome and its metabolic potential in these environments. Palsa soils hosted microbial communities that were distinct from the peatland soils, ponds and stream waters. Soils and their adjacent ponds shared taxa, and peat soils and ponds also shared taxa with the streams, unlike palsa soils which were likely more hydrologically isolated. Based on metabolic marker gene ratio for denitrification [(nirS+nirK)/nosZ], palsa soils had the strongest potential to be a source of nitrous oxide (N2O) out of all the sample types. Marker genes for hydrogen gas (H2) and carbon monoxide (CO) utilization were abundant across soil environments and metagenome assembled genomes, suggesting that trace gas oxidation is likely one of the key energy sources for microbiomes. In the palsa ponds and peat soils pmoA, marker gene for methanotrophy, was the most abundant compared to the other sample types.

### Exploring the Genetic Diversity and Ecological Niche Breadth of Bradyrhizobium in Tropical Ecosystems

Bradyrhizobium exhibits exceptional genetic versatility, functional adaptability, and evolutionary resilience, serving as a critical stabilizing agent in tropical ecosystems. However, its full potential remains underexplored, largely because soil health assessments have historically relied on temperate region and studies often analyze traits in isolation rather than tracking genomic responses to combined environmental stresses. This review applies the niche breadth triangle framework to synthesize the genomic structure, evolutionary history, and multi stress tolerance of tropical Bradyrhizobium lineages. We traced the evolutionary trajectory of the strains, detailing how ancestral lineages fist adapted to to acidic, metal rich soils before acquiring mobile symbiosis islands and specialized secretion systems that broaden their host range. In addition, we highlight that tropical lineages harbor an expansive accessory genome of ~39% variable accessory genes, showcasing a significantly more promising adaptive potential than temperate Bradyrhizobium species. Key protective mechanisms reviewed include modified heat shock promoters, robust extracellular matrices, and photosynthetic metabolism, which preserve nitrogenase activity during drought, high temperatures, and flooding. High strain diversity ensures functional redundancy and niche complementarity, maintaining consistent N-fixation under pressure. Consequently, this work demonstrates how local management practices and stress resistant strains can improve rhizosphere conditions that simultaneously building climate resilient agricultural systems.

### Contrasting One-Season Responses of Soybean Yield and Rhizosphere Bacterial Communities to Tillage and Straw Management in a Salt-Affected Farmland in Southern Xinjiang

In salt-affected soil, different agronomic practices can alter crop productivity and rhizosphere microbial dynamics, yet whether their short-term responses are consistent remains unclear. A single-site, one-season field experiment was conducted in 2025 in Zepu County, southern Xinjiang, China. Three tillage treatments, conventional tillage (CT), no-tillage (NT), and no-tillage with straw mulching (NT&SM), were compared for soybean yield, rhizosphere soil properties, and bacterial communities. The results showed that the soybean yield under CT was significantly higher than that under NT&SM. Contrary to the yield trend, the NT&SM treatment exhibited the highest mean values for the Chao1 and Shannon indices (the overall treatment effect was significant). PERMANOVA based on Bray–Curtis distance indicated that short-term tillage significantly altered the rhizosphere bacterial community structure, explaining 34.6% of the variation in composition. Tillage practices produced different rhizosphere bacterial community profiles. However, these short-term shifts in bacterial diversity and composition did not parallel the yield response. This suggests that both crop productivity and rhizosphere ecological responses should be considered simultaneously when evaluating conservation tillage practices. These findings represent short-term responses under the conditions of a single site and growing season and require validation through multi-year, multi-site experiments.

### As Below, So Above?: Ecology and Diversification of Mycoparasitic Fungi in the Rhizosphere and Phyllosphere

The repository contains the ITS sequence data and files used to prepare the alignment for phylogenetic analysis. ITS.fasta contains the original sequences. The sequences were aligned with MAFFT using the default settings and automatic strategy selection through the interactive terminal interface, producing ITS_mafft.fasta. The alignment was then trimmed with trimAl using the command trimal -in ITS_mafft.fasta -out ITS_trimal.fasta -gt 0.9 -cons 60, producing ITS_trimal.fasta.
