## 农田土壤微生物

### Organic-mineral amendments enhance soil carbon accumulation and rice biomass in coastal salinized soil by regulating fungal necromass carbon and microbial communities

Introduction Coastal soil salinization impairs soil structure and microbial functionality, thereby hindering soil organic carbon (SOC) accumulation and constraining rice productivity. Organic amendments offer a promising route for reclaiming such degradedsoil; however, the mechanisms by which they modulate soil carbon transformation dynamics and rhizosphere microbial communities to improve rice productivity remain poorly resolved. Methods To address this gap, we conducted a pot experiment on coastal saline soil with four treatments: (i) conventional chemical fertilizer (NPK), (ii) NPK combined with attapulgite (ATG), (iii) NPK + ATG + composted straw/swine manure (ATG.SM), and (iv) NPK + ATG + woody peat (ATG.WP). Results Results showed that, compared with NPK, ATG.WP significantly mitigated soil salinity, as indicated by 26.0%, 25.8%, and 9.3% reductions in electrical conductivity (EC), total salt content (TSC), and sodium adsorption ratio (SAR), respectively, alongside a 12.2% increase in soil Ca 2+ and elevated rice biomass. ATG.WP also significantly increased SOC, particulate organic C (POC), microbial biomass C (MBC), total amino sugars, fungal necromass C (FNC), the fungal-to-bacterial necromass C ratio (F/B), and C management index (CMI) ( p < 0.05). Activities of cellobiohydrolase (CBH) and b-glucosidase (bG) enzymes were 163.8% and 81.5% higher under ATG.WP than under NPK. Correlation analysis revealed that rice biomass was negatively correlated with soil salinity indicators but positively correlated with multiple soil C components and C-cycling enzyme activities. Metagenomics analysis further demonstrated that ATG.WP selectively enriched beneficial bacterial phyla (e.g., Pseudomonadota, Chloroflexota, Acidobacteriota), while concurrently suppressed fungal taxa such as Ascomycota and Chytridiomycota. Random forest modeling identified FNC and POC as the primary predictors of SOC accumulation, and structural equation modeling (SEM) revealed that organic-mineral amendments enhanced SOC accumulation by alleviating saline stress, stimulating fungal necromass C production, and recruiting specialized bacterial consortia that chemically stabilize the labile C component. Discussion Collectively, these findings demonstrate that co-application of woody peat and attapulgite is an effective organic-mineral strategy for reclaiming coastal saline paddy soil, operating through a fungal-dominated microbial carbon pump that drives stable SOC accumulation.

### Shifts in Soil Microbial Communities Under Cover Cropping in Citrus Orchards

Cover crops are increasingly used by growers in the United States as an effective approach to enhance soil health. The interactions between cover crops and soil microbiomes have been studied extensively; however, research evaluating the impact of cover crops on rhizosphere microbial communities in tree crop systems, particularly under nutrient-limited field soils in Florida, remains limited. To address these knowledge gaps, a three-year field study was conducted to evaluate the effects of cover crop mixtures on soil bacterial and fungal communities in orchards of different citrus species and cultivars across seasons. The trial included ‘Star Ruby’ grapefruit trees grafted on ‘US-942’, ‘Bearss’ lemon trees grafted on ‘Sour Orange’, and ‘OLL-8’ sweet orange trees grafted on ‘US-942’ rootstock in a randomized complete block design comparing conventional management (no cover crop-control) with cover crop treatments. Bacterial community composition showed consistent taxonomic patterns across cultivars and treatments, with Proteobacteria consistently the most abundant phylum across grapefruit, lemon, and orange orchards, followed by Actinobacteriota, Acidobacteriota, and Firmicutes. Despite this overall stability, cultivar was the strongest determinant of bacterial community structure. Additionally, functional analysis revealed significant treatment-associated shifts in predicted guilds related to nitrogen transformations, carbon metabolism, sulfur oxidation, and manganese oxidation. In contrast, fungal community composition and predicted functional profiles were primarily structured by season, with limited effects of cultivar and cover cropping. Fungal communities were consistently dominated by Ascomycota across orchards, sampling dates, and treatments.

### Geographic Environment Outweighs Halophyte Identity in Shaping Nitrogen-Cycling Microbial Communities in Coastal Saline Soils

Halophytes are thought to recruit nitrogen (N)-cycling microorganisms that alleviate nutrient limitation in coastal saline soils, yet the relative importance of geographic environment versus halophyte identity in structuring these communities remains unclear. We sampled rhizosphere soils of eight plant species (18 species × site combinations) at four coastal sites in the Yellow River Delta and Laizhou Bay, China. Two sites had low salinity (0.56–0.71‰) and two had high salinity (8.48–11.40‰). The abundances of ammonia-oxidizing archaea (AOA, arch-amoA gene) and bacteria (AOB, amoA gene) and denitrifiers (nirS, nirK, nosZ) were quantified via qPCR. AOA, AOB, and nosZ-type denitrifier communities were profiled using amplicon sequencing. The geographic location significantly structured all three communities (PERMANOVA R2 = 0.65–0.68, p < 0.01), whereas plant traits did not; salinity, moisture, pH, and total carbon were the main correlates. High-salinity sites harbored ~14-fold more nirS copies and higher AOA diversity than low-salinity sites, with dominance shifting from Nitrososphaera to Nitrosopumilus in AOA and from Nitrosovibrio to Nitrosospira in AOB. Plant-associated effects were weaker and limited to gene abundance and α-diversity. Secretohalophytes harbored the highest AOA abundance, and perennials had ~10-fold more nosZ copies than annuals. Our results contribute to improving knowledge on different effects of geographic environment and host plant traits on the N-cycling microbial assembly in coastal saline soils.

### Comparative multi-omics of host genome, metabolome and associated rhizosphere microbiota across karst-endemic and widespread Rhododendron species

Abstract Background To examine how classically calcifuge plants persist in phosphorus-poor, high-Ca 2+ karst bare-rock habitats, we compared genome, leaf-metabolome and associated rhizosphere-microbiome patterns in the karst-endemic Rhododendron bailiense Y.P.Ma, C.Q.Zhang & D.F.Chamb. with those in the widespread R. delavayi Franch. and allopatric R. ambiguum Hemsl. We integrated a multi-omics investigation across three natural microhabitats to decode these inter-kingdom interactions, combining whole-genome comparative analysis, UHPLC-MS/MS non-targeted leaf metabolomics, fungal internal transcribed spacer (ITS) amplicon profiling, and deep rhizosphere metagenomic sequencing. Results Comparative genomics revealed significant expansion of secondary metabolism gene families in R. bailiense . Metabolomics showed a distinct chemical profile rich in specific flavonoids and polyacetylenes, such as luteolinidin and safynol. These species-specific metabolite profiles co-varied with microbial hubs, including Piloderma , Streptomyces , Pseudorhodoplanes and Candidatus Sulfotelmatobacter , whose reported or predicted functions are relevant to phosphorus mobilization, rhizosphere defence, recalcitrant-carbon turnover and mineral-associated sulfur cycling. In contrast, the R. delavayi and R. ambiguum rhizospheres were enriched in saprotrophic taxa, including Mortierella , consistent with broader nutrient-turnover potential. Conclusions R. bailiense showed a species-specific leaf-metabolite profile that co-varied with a distinctive rhizosphere microbiome. Together, these coordinated patterns suggest a potential functional link between species-specific host chemistry and rhizosphere-community organization, and highlight microbial and metabolic candidates relevant to karst persistence and conservation.
