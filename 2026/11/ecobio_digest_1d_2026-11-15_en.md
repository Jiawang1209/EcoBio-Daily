### Depth-Dependent Responses of Soil Microbial Communities to Nitrogen Application Gradients Under Rice Straw Return

Crop straw return is widely used to maintain soil fertility in intensive rice systems. How soil microbial communities respond to nitrogen rate under full straw return, and whether these responses differ between soil layers, remains poorly resolved. In this study, a field experiment with five nitrogen application rates (0 [RF0], 53 [RF1], 93 [RF2], 133 [RF3], and 173 [RF4] kg N ha−1) under uniform full rice straw return (7500 kg straw ha−1) was conducted in a cold-region paddy ecosystem. Soil physicochemical properties, bacterial community structure and functional potential (metagenomics) were systematically analyzed in surface (0–10 cm) and subsurface (10–20 cm) layers. Results showed that moderate nitrogen application (133 kg N ha−1) optimally enhanced total nitrogen, total organic carbon, labile carbon fractions, and available phosphorus and potassium in both soil layers. However, excessive nitrogen application (173 kg N ha−1) provided no further benefits and significantly decreased available phosphorus and potassium relative to their peak values observed under optimal nitrogen inputs. A combined PCoA of both soil layers showed no separation by depth or by nitrogen rate. PERMANOVA detected a weak but significant overall model (R2 = 0.241, p = 0.0002) without a significant depth × nitrogen interaction (R2= 0.065, p = 0.328). Bacterial taxonomic composition exhibited strong resistance to nitrogen disturbance, with a large core microbiome (dominated by Proteobacteria, Chloroflexi, and Acidobacteria) remaining stable across treatments and depths. In contrast, functional genes and metabolic pathways involved in carbon, nitrogen, and sulfur cycling displayed high plasticity. Moderate nitrogen activated complex carbohydrate degradation and dark carbon fixation in deeper soil, while high nitrogen shifted surface communities from autotrophic carbon fixation (Calvin-Benson-Bassham cycle) toward heterotrophic fermentation and strongly stimulated denitrification pathways, increasing potential gaseous nitrogen loss. Overall, this study reveals a clear taxonomic-functional decoupling and demonstrates that 133 kg N ha−1 represents the optimal threshold for balancing soil nutrient accumulation, microbial functional activity, and environmental risk under full rice straw return. These findings provide a theoretical basis for precision nitrogen management in straw-return systems.

### Nitrogen Fertilizer Reduction with Organic Residue Incorporation Mitigates Soil Organic Carbon Mineralisation by Regulating Microbial Community and CAZyme Genes

The co-application of organic matter and nitrogen (N) fertiliser has been recommended as an environmentally friendly practice. However, excessive N fertiliser use contributes to carbon (C) loss and several environmental problems. Our study aims to analyse the effects of reducing mineral N application under supplemental organic residue on organic C mineralisation and its microbial mechanisms. Therefore, a 120-day incubation experiment was conducted to assess the effects of incorporating 13C-labelled pruning branch residue combined with different N levels (225, BRN1; 180, BRN2; 160, BRN3; and 140 mg N·kg−1, BRN4) on CO2 emission, soil microbial community and functional genes in apple orchard soil. All treatments increased CO2 efflux and induced a positive priming effect (PE), expressed as promoted native soil organic C mineralisation. BRN1 produced the highest positive PE, followed by BRN2 and BRN4, and the lowest PE was observed under BRN3. We highlight the importance of plant- and bacteria-derived biomass (indicated by peptidoglycan) decomposition rather than the composition and structure of microbial communities in regulating organic C decomposition in response to pruning residue and N addition. In addition, reduced N treatments decreased abundance of C-degrading genes for plant-derived (hemicellulose, lignin) and bacteria-derived biomass (peptidoglycan) as compared with BRN1. Four near-complete bacterial genome bins were strongly correlated with PE; three of these bins were assigned to Actinomycetota and one to Acidobacteriota. Moreover, structural equation modelling indicated CAZyme-encoding genes had a stronger association with PE than microbial community. Collectively, coupling branch residue with N reduction has great potential for mitigating soil C loss. The optimal amount of N reduction should be further evaluated based on the fruit yield in the field.

### Data for "Nitrous oxide emissions from terrestrial systems and the partial contributions of nitrification and denitrification"

Nitrous oxide (N2O) is a potent greenhouse gas, yet the partitioning of N2O emissions from nitrification and denitrification has not been systematically updated. Moreover, existing estimates of N2O emissions from these two processes generally do not account for nitric oxide (NO), potentially resulting in an overestimation of N2O emissions. In the present study, we updated the fraction of N2O emissions from nitrification and the ratio of its emissions from denitrification and investigated the environmental factors regulating these parameters based on a comprehensive meta dataset. In contrast to previous meta-analyses and their representation in process-based models, both the fraction and ratio varied among climate types, land covers, and soil textures, as well as their interactions. Notably, the ratio was negatively associated with soil temperature, pH, C/N ratio, and soil moisture. Based on identified environmental drivers, we used machine learning to estimate the global distributions of the fraction and ratio. By combining literature-derived global nitrification and denitrification rates, the reported proportion of NO produced during denitrification, and the globally predicted fractions and ratios obtained in this study, N2O emissions from nitrification and denitrification were estimated at 0.041±0.096 and 1.47±2.4 mg kg-1 day-1, respectively. Accounting for NO emissions from denitrification reduced estimated daily N2O emissions from denitrification by approximately 6.18%. Overall, this study provides quantitative evidence for improving the representation of N2O production and partitioning in models. Future studies should evaluate the performance of the results identified in the present study using independent datasets before their broader application in process-based models.

### Fresh tephra deposits from the Tajogaite Volcano boost thermophile proliferation and soil organic matter recovery

Tephra fallout deposition during volcanic eruptions overlays existing soils, profoundly altering their physical, chemical, and biological properties. This study investigates the impact of the newly deposited tephra blanket from the 2021 Tajogaite eruption (La Palma Island) on the molecular composition of soil organic matter and microbial diversity across different soil horizons. To our knowledge, this is the first study combining molecular pyrolysis, stable isotope analysis, and microbial sequencing to characterize the early biogeochemical transformation of soils affected by the Tajogaite eruption. A combination of 16S rRNA gene sequencing, pyrolysis-gas chromatography/mass spectrometry (Py-GC/MS), and elemental and isotope ratio mass spectrometry (EA/IRMS) was employed. Our results reveal marked differences in organic matter composition and microbial community structure across the tephra-covered soil profile, with patterns consistent with ongoing microbial transformation of organic carbon and nitrogen compounds. The soil horizon directly beneath the tephra layer (horizon O) displayed a higher abundance of labile organic compounds and a reduced presence of recalcitrant compounds compared to the deeper horizons (A and Bw). This pattern is strongly associated with the predominance of thermophilic bacteria, which contribute actively to the breakdown of complex organic materials such as lignin and hydrocarbons, and drive key biogeochemical processes including nitrogen and carbon cycling. The continuous geothermal influence of nearby fumaroles further supports the persistence and ecological success of thermophilic communities in these volcanic soils. These findings underscore the critical role of volcanic activity not only in reshaping soil structure but also in enhancing soil fertility and resilience through microbial-mediated processes. Understanding these dynamics is essential for soil management and ecosystem recovery strategies in volcanic regions, providing new insights into the long-term effects of tephra deposition on soil health and the carbon cycle.

### Predominance of viral core functions over auxiliary metabolism among carbohydrate-active enzymes annotated in the global soil virosphere

Soil viruses are increasingly recognized as components of microbial communities with the potential to alter ecosystem functioning, yet the frequency and functional distribution of virus-encoded metabolic genes in soils remain poorly understood. Here, the Global Soil Virus Atlas gene catalog, comprising 1,432,147 viral genes from 1,223 samples across 12 ecosystem types, was searched against KEGG, Pfam, and CAZy for functions related to carbon cycling, nitrogen cycling, and antimicrobial resistance. Annotations associated with host entry or cell-wall lysis were separated from putative auxiliary metabolic functions. Of 5,020 initial gene-level functional annotations across the three categories, 3,322 (66.2%) were classified as peptidoglycanases. Their exclusion left 1,698 genes (0.14% of the gene catalog restricted to contigs ≥10 kb), and carbon-cycling functions represented 96.7% of the enzyme-resolved annotations. Chitinase-associated genes formed the largest carbon category, but 78.0% belonged to GH19, a family containing both chitinases and phage endolysins. Among GH19 genes on contigs encoding detectable holins or spanins, 84.2% occurred within three genes of these lysis components, a distribution comparable to that of canonical phage lysozymes. Across six matched metagenomic studies, estimated viral contributions to the targeted functions ranged from 0.00% to 8.45% (mean: 2.27%). Two of the three estimates exceeding 2% were GH19-associated chitinase estimates from rhizosphere soils and were therefore treated as upper estimates. Thus, the putative soil viral metabolic inventory was sparse and concentrated in carbon-degradation functions, whereas much of the initial carbohydrate-active enzyme signal reflected core viral lysis functions rather than auxiliary metabolism. IMPORTANCE: Soil microorganisms drive decomposition and nutrient cycling, and the viruses that infect them may alter these processes by carrying genes that affect host metabolism. However, genes annotated as carbohydrate-degradation enzymes may instead help viruses enter cells or break host cell walls when new virus particles are released. In a worldwide catalog of soil viruses, approximately two-thirds of the initial functional assignments represented these infection or release activities. After their removal, putative viral auxiliary metabolic genes were uncommon and concentrated mainly in carbon degradation. Genes potentially involved in chitin degradation were most frequent, but many occurred near viral genes used to break host cells; their estimated contribution should therefore be viewed as an upper limit. Distinguishing core viral functions from host metabolic modification is essential to avoid overestimating viral contributions to soil processes and to identify the strongest candidates for experimental testing.

### Strawberry Rhizosphere Soil Microorganism Sequencing

16S rRNA and ITS amplicon sequencing performed on strawberry rhizosphere soil microorganisms. In this study, rhizosphere soil samples attached to the root surface of cultivated strawberry plants were collected in the field under standardized sampling specifications. We extracted total microbial genomic DNA from each soil sample with commercial DNA extraction kits, detected DNA concentration and purity through agarose gel electrophoresis and ultraviolet spectrophotometry to ensure qualified DNA quality for library construction. Targeted amplification was conducted on the V3 and V4 hypervariable region of bacterial 16S rRNA gene and the ITS1 region of fungal internal transcribed spacer respectively. After PCR product purification, quantification and homogenization, high throughput sequencing was carried out on the Illumina sequencing platform. Raw sequencing reads will be deposited into public omics databases for permanent open access; subsequent bioinformatic analyses including sequence quality trimming, operational taxonomic unit clustering, species annotation, alpha and beta diversity calculation, and differential species screening will be implemented to reveal the composition, abundance and interaction patterns of bacterial and fungal communities in strawberry rhizosphere soil. The publicly available raw sequencing data can be reused by peer researchers for meta analysis of crop rhizosphere microbiomes, mechanism exploration of plantmicrobe interaction and screening of beneficial microbial strains for strawberry cultivation.

### Parental effects can be transmitted through the rhizosphere microbiome

Plant responses to environmental changes, including to competition by neighboring plants, can be influenced by soil microbes. They can also be modified through parental effects, which can transmit environmental cues across generations and shape plant phenotypes through various mechanisms such as epigenetic modifications. However, we do not yet know whether parental effects in plants can also act through modifications of the rhizosphere microbiota. Here, we grew eight genotypes of Phragmites australis with or without interspecific competition, and examined its transgenerational effects on offspring phenotype and rhizosphere microbial communities in a common environment. We found that competition decreased plant growth in a genotype-specific manner, but plants whose parents had been exposed to competition previously shifted their functional phenotypes towards a more conservative resource strategy. Notably, parental competition also altered the diversity and community structure of rhizosphere soil bacteria and fungi in offspring plants, and structural equation models indicated that these soil microbiome changes may have driven some of the observed changes in offspring phenotypes. Our study demonstrates that parental environmental effects can influence offspring traits not only directly, but also through alteration of rhizosphere microorganisms, and that parental effects are more complex than we previously knew.
