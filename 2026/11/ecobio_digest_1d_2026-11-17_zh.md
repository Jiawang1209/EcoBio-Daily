### Effects of spent stropharia rugosoannulata mushroom substrate incorporation on soil nutrients and microbial community diversity

To investigate the effect of spent substrate incorporation following Stropharia rugosoannulata cultivation on soil fertility and soil microecological environment, a field experiment was conducted, with uncultivated soil without S. rugosoannulata as the control (CK). The responses of soil nutrient contents, bacterial and fungal community structures, diversity patterns, and community assembly mechanisms to the spent mushroom substrate incorporation treatment (W) were analyzed. The results showed that: soil alkali-hydrolyzable nitrogen (AN) and available phosphorus (AP) contents in treatment (W) were significantly increased by 23.82% and 38.86%, respectively (P <0.05). Soil organic matter (SOM) and available potassium (AK) showed non-significant increasing trends, while soil pH decreased slightly. Alpha diversity analysis indicated that treatment W reduced species richness (sobs, Chao1 index) and community diversity (Shannon index) of both bacterial and fungal communities; the reductions were non-significant for bacteria. Community composition analysis revealed that treatment W markedly altered soil microbial community structure. At the phylum level, the relative abundance of Actinobacteriota was significantly enriched among the bacteria, whereas Mortierellomycota abundance was greatly elevated in case of fungi. At the genus level, beneficial microorganisms including Mortierella and Penicillium increased in relative abundance, while potential phytopathogens such as Fusarium declined. Community assembly analysis based on βNTI indicated that deterministic processes, particularly homogeneous selection, dominated microbial community assembly under treatment W. In conclusion, incorporation of spent substrate from S. rugosoannulata cultivation can effectively elevate soil available nutrient levels, optimize soil microbial community structure, enrich beneficial microbes, suppress pathogens, and improve soil ecological functions. This practice represents a promising green soil amendment strategy for agricultural soil improvement. Bangladesh J. Bot. 55(3): 695-710, 2026 (September) Special

### Effects of reductive soil disinfestation and fertilizer reduction on soil health, tobacco performance and microbial communities under continuous cropping

A field experiment was conducted in a long-term tobacco monoculture field to evaluate the effects of reductive soil disinfestation (RSD) using graded corn straw doses combined with reduced fertilizer application on soil quality and microbial environment of continuously cropped tobacco soil in the karst region of Guizhou. Five treatments were set including blank control (CK), flooding plus mulching without straw (T0), and RSD with 400 kg/mu (T1), 800 kg/mu (T2), 1200 kg/mu (T3) corn straw. The responses of soil physicochemical properties, tobacco agronomic traits, disease incidence, economic characteristics, leaf chemical quality, and soil bacterial and fungal community structure to different RSD treatments were systematically analyzed. The results showed that RSD treatments significantly increased soil pH and alleviated soil acidification and notably elevated the contents of soil alkali-hydrolyzable nitrogen (AN), available phosphorus (AP), available potassium (AK) and organic matter (SOM), among which T2 treatment presented the optimal improvement effect. RSD treatments significantly optimized tobacco agronomic traits at vigorous growth, budding and topping stages, effectively reduced the incidence and disease index of tobacco black root rot, and greatly improved tobacco yield, output value and chemical quality coordination, with the best performance in T2 treatment. RSD treatments dramatically reshaped soil microbial community structure. At the phylum level, RSD enriched beneficial microbial phyla and inhibited potential pathogenic phyla; at the genus level, the relative abundance of beneficial genera such as Trichoderma and Penicillium increased, while the pathogenic genus Fusarium decreased significantly. Correlation analysis revealed that soil physicochemical properties were closely correlated with microbial community composition, and RSD treatments enhanced soil nutrient cycling-related microbial functions. In conclusion, RSD with 800 kg/mu corn straw combined with fertilizer reduction can effectively ameliorate soil physicochemical properties, suppress soil-borne pathogens, optimize microbial community structure and improve tobacco yield and quality. This approach offers a feasible green remediation strategy for managing soil degradation under tobacco monoculture in karst regions. Bangladesh J. Bot. 55(3): 727-739, 2026 (September) Special

### Exploring the Potential of Indigenous Plant-Associated Microorganisms for Sustainable Bioremediation of Heavy Metal-Contaminated Environments

Heavy metal contamination has become a persistent environmental concern, particularly in regions affected by industrial development, mining, urban expansion, and intensive agricultural practices. Unlike many organic pollutants, metallic contaminants cannot be broken down into harmless substances through conventional biological degradation. Their persistence, potential mobility, and accumulation in living organisms create substantial challenges for environmental management. Conventional remediation methods, although effective under certain conditions, are often expensive, resource-intensive, and disruptive to soil ecosystems. Consequently, biological remediation strategies involving plants and their associated microorganisms have attracted increasing scientific interest. Indigenous microorganisms inhabiting the rhizosphere, root tissues, and other plant compartments represent a promising biological resource because they are frequently adapted to local environmental conditions. These organisms can influence metal availability through biosorption, bioaccumulation, precipitation, oxidation–reduction reactions, and the production of extracellular metabolites. Certain plant-associated bacteria an d fungi also improve plant establishment under metal stress by supporting nutrient acquisition, regulating stress-related physiological responses, and modifying rhizosphere conditions. This review critically examines the ecological significance, functional characteristics, and biotechnological potential of indigenous plant-associated microorganisms in the remediation of heavy metal-contaminated environments. Particular attention is given to rhizobacteria, endophytic bacteria, and mycorrhizal fungi, together with their interactions with host plants. The review further considers the limitations of laboratory-based investigations, challenges associated with field application, and the importance of integrating microbial ecology with environmental monitoring. Evidence from the available literature suggests that locally adapted plant–microbe associations may provide a practical foundation for sustainable remediation, provided that their effectiveness, environmental safety, and long-term stability are demonstrated under realistic field conditions
