### AZOTOBACTER-BASED BIOFERTILIZERS: MECHANISM OF PLANT GROWTH PROMOTION, AGRICULTURAL APPLICATIONS AND FUTURE PERSPECTIVES

The increasing use of chemical fertilizers has raised serious environmental concerns, highlighting the need for sustainable and eco-friendly alternatives. Azotobacter spp. are free-living, nitrogen-fixing bacteria that play an important role in improving soil fertility and promoting plant growth. These bacteria convert atmospheric nitrogen into biologically available forms and enhance plant development through the production of phytohormones, phosphate solubilization, siderophore production, and biocontrol activities. This review discusses the classification, structural features, nitrogen fixation process, and plant growth-promoting activities of Azotobacter species. It also discusses their applications as biofertilizers in sustainable agriculture, particularly their ability to improve nutrient availability, support root development, enhance crop productivity, and contribute to soil health. Furthermore, the comparative advantages of Azotobacter over other beneficial bacteria, including Rhizobium, Azospirillum, Bacillus, and Pseudomonas, are highlighted. Beyond agriculture, Azotobacter has attracted attention for its pharmaceutical and industrial applications. Its production of biopolymers such as alginate and polyhydroxybutyrate (PHB) offers potential applications in drug delivery systems, wound dressings, tissue engineering, and biodegradable materials. Other valuable products, including vitamins, melanin, enzymes, and biosurfactants, further demonstrate its biotechnological importance. Despite these promising applications, challenges such as inconsistent field performance, formulation limitations, storage stability, and inadequate farmer awareness restrict its widespread adoption. Future research focusing on genetic improvement, advanced formulation techniques, nano-encapsulation, and microbial co-inoculation may enhance its effectiveness and commercial potential. Overall, Azotobacter represents a multifunctional microorganism with considerable promise for sustainable agriculture, pharmaceutical biotechnology, and environmentally responsible industrial development.

### Management rather than climate and biodiversity shapes long-term delta15N dynamics in grassland soils

The bulk soil δ 15 N signature in grassland soils serves as a fingerprint of nitrogen inputs, turnover and losses, reflecting the influence of land management, climate, plant biodiversity and abiotic soil properties.Although natural abundance δ 15 N has been proposed as an indicator for these factors, previous studies yielded mixed results regarding the importance of its drivers.In this study, we quantified changes in soil δ 15 N across 72 German grassland sites after 12 years (2011 vs. 2023) and analysed relationships with detailed records of land use intensity, plant biodiversity, climate, and soil properties.Land use intensity was quantified on a continuous scale based on mowing, grazing and fertilisation.In the top 10 cm of soil, δ 15 N changes ranged from -0.86 to + 2.49‰, equating to an average increase of + 0.35‰ per decade.Over two-thirds of our study sites showed a long-term increase in δ 15 N associated with high land use intensity.Our findings indicate that fertiliser application and grazing are the dominant drivers of δ 15 N increases, while a biodiversity-mediated decrease via reduced N losses was not observed.The slow decadal-scale shifts suggest that single soil δ 15 N measurements without known initial values are only informative for long-term management processes and not for assessing recent management impacts.
