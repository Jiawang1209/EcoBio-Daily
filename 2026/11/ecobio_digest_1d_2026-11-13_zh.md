### ROLE OF BIOFERTILIZERS IN IMPROVING SOIL HEALTH AND SUSTAINABLE AGRICULTURE

Abstract: Rapid population growth and the intensification of industrial and agricultural activities have increased pressure on global food systems, demanding environmentally responsible strategies to sustain crop productivity. Biofertilizers microbial inoculants capable of improving nutrient availability through processes such as nitrogen fixation, phosphate solubilization, potassium mobilization, and organic matter decomposition have emerged as key tools in sustainable agriculture. These beneficial microbes enhance soil fertility, strengthen plant growth, and support long-term ecosystem health while reducing dependence on synthetic fertilizers that contribute to soil degradation, water pollution, and greenhouse gas emissions. Major groups of biofertilizers include nitrogen-fixing bacteria (e.g., Rhizobium, Azospirillum, Azotobacter), phosphate- and potassium-solubilizing microorganisms, sulfur- and zinc-mobilizers, mycorrhizal fungi, and decomposers such as Trichoderma. Their application has been shown to improve nutrient uptake, root development, crop yield, and plant resilience to abiotic stress. As global food demand continues to rise, integrating biofertilizers into modern farming systems offers a viable pathway to enhance crop productivity, restore soil health, and promote environmentally sustainable agricultural practices.

### WHEAT PRODUCTIVITY AND SOIL HEALTH: THE ROLE OF AZOTOBACTER AND ORGANIC AMENDMENTS

Abstract: Wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) is one of the world‘s most important cereal crops, serving as a major source of calories and proteins for humans. However, modern intensive farming practices have caused severe soil degradation, nutrient imbalance, and declining productivity. To restore soil fertility and maintain sustainable wheat production, bio-fertilizers such as Azotobacter and organic amendments (FYM, compost, and vermicompost) play a crucial role. These natural inputs not only enhance soil microbial activity and nutrient cycling but also improve soil structure and plant health. This chapter discusses the synergistic effects of Azotobacter and organic amendments on soil fertility, crop yield, and long-term agricultural sustainability. ### Chemical warfare in the rice plant rhizosphere: Diverse Pseudomonas antagonize the green alga Chlamydomonas

Algae and bacteria form dynamic partnerships that shape diverse ecosystems. The model green microalga Chlamydomonas reinhardtii is often found in paddy rice fields, where the diversity and functional characteristics of its bacterial partners remain largely unexplored. Here, we isolated 12 bacterial strains from the rice rhizosphere and its surrounding soil. Phylogenetic analysis based on 16S rRNA gene sequences revealed that eight isolates belong to the genus Pseudomonas. Three Pseudomonas rhizosphere strains exhibited antagonistic effects against C. reinhardtii, negatively impacting its growth, pigmentation and cell morphology. Close physical associations between the three antagonistic Pseudomonas strains and C. reinhardtii were observed in liquid cultures. Whole genome sequencing identified the antagonistic strains as Pseudomonas alloputida, Pseudomonas tohonis, and Pseudomonas asiatica. The three bacterial genomes encode several factors well-known for mediating chemical warfare. Notably, P. tohonis bears a gene cluster encoding the phenolic toxin, 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol (DAPG). DAPG inhibits the growth and alters the cell morphology of C. reinhardtii. These findings expand our understanding of the diversity of bacterial partners associated with C. reinhardtii and highlight the role of Pseudomonas rice rhizosphere-associated bacteria as potent green algal antagonists. They harbor a reservoir of common functional traits that negatively influence green algal population dynamics.

### CONSERVATION OF POLLINATORS AND SOIL MICROBIOTA: INTERCONNECTED STRATEGIES FOR ECOSYSTEM HEALTH

Abstract: The conservation of pollinators and soil microbiota represents a critical frontier in maintaining global ecosystem stability and agricultural productivity. While traditionally studied separately, emerging research reveals profound interconnections between these two fundamental components of terrestrial ecosystems. This chapter synthesizes evidence demonstrating how soil microbial communities influence pollinator health indirectly through mediating plant nutrition and floral resource quality, while pollinators contribute to soil health by transporting microbial communities and supporting plant reproduction. The integrated conservation approach grounded in the "One Health" framework recognizes that the vitality of agricultural systems, natural environments, and human well-being are inextricably linked. By examining the threats posed by agricultural intensification, habitat loss, and chemical inputs, this chapter identifies synergistic conservation strategies including pollinator-friendly farming practices, soil microbiome management, and policy initiatives that simultaneously support both groups. The synthesis concludes that future conservation efforts must adopt this interconnected perspective to develop effective ecosystem management protocols that enhance resilience, maintain biodiversity, and secure essential ecosystem services. Keywords: Pollinator Conservation, Soil Microbiome, Ecological Networks, Agricultural Sustainability, Ecosystem Services
